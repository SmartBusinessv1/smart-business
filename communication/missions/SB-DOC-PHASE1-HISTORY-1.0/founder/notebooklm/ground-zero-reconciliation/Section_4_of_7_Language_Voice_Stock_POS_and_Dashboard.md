# Part A — Human Language Layer

## 2. Human Language Layer — enduring Founder intent

The Human Language Layer is not merely translation. It is the product layer that allows Smart Business to understand people in the way Kerala merchants, employees, suppliers, customers, and delivery staff actually communicate.

The recurring historical intent is:

- English, Malayalam, and Manglish must all be first-class interaction modes;
- Malayalam may appear in native script or transliterated into Latin characters;
- users should not need formal grammar, accounting vocabulary, ERP terminology, or standardized data-entry syntax;
- language preference should be remembered and applied consistently;
- the system should understand local business vocabulary, names, units, shorthand, and mixed-language speech;
- responses should preserve meaning and business context rather than perform literal translation;
- the language layer should work across WhatsApp, voice, dashboard, reminders, Ask CFO, support, HR, supplier workflows, customer workflows, and delivery workflows;
- ambiguity must trigger clarification rather than guessing.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT`

The current product principle remains: technology adapts to people; people should not be forced to adapt to technology.

## 3. Real Kerala retail language conditions

Ground Zero is especially valuable because it describes the actual communication environment the Founder was designing for:

- rapid speech;
- clipped sentences;
- local accents;
- mixed Malayalam and English;
- Manglish spellings that vary from user to user;
- noisy counters, fans, traffic, printers, customers, and kitchen/shop-floor sounds;
- abbreviations such as packets, bags, cases, kilos and local shorthand;
- colloquial business terms for income, expense, credit, repayment, stock, supplier and cash;
- names with spelling variation;
- quantities such as `2k`, `50 pkt`, `3 chaku`;
- incomplete statements that may omit purpose or another important field;
- statements where one word/person may match multiple business identities.

Examples recovered include variants of:

- `Supplier-nu boxes-inu 1500 koduthu`;
- `Milma-yk ayirathi anjooru koduthu`;
- `PVP-nn 50 pkt paal, 3 chaku panchasara stock ketti`;
- `Ravi-ku 3500 chelavu`;
- `Rinesh present 500` as an intentionally ambiguous example.

The enduring design rule is not to depend on any one fixed slang dictionary. The system should use context, Business Memory, role, recent conversation, known parties/items, and explicit clarification where needed.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

Historical fixed dictionaries, exact phonetic prompt strings, hardcoded confidence thresholds such as `0.85`, and exact claims such as `99.8% precision` are implementation provenance, not current guarantees.

## 4. Clarification and ambiguity

The strongest durable behaviour is:

> If Smart Business cannot safely determine the user's meaning, it should say what it understood, identify the ambiguity, and ask the shortest useful clarification before writing consequential business data.

For example, if `Rinesh present 500` could mean an attendance-related amount or a customer-credit transaction, the system should not choose one silently.

Historical Ground Zero often routed ambiguity into security quarantine. Current architecture should distinguish:

- ordinary language ambiguity;
- business validation issue;
- system/parser failure;
- genuine security/abuse event.

Ordinary uncertainty is not automatically a security incident.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

**Classification:** `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT`

## 6. Voice packaging evolution

The historical evidence reveals three layers that were sometimes conflated:

### Basic Voice Assistant

Historically intended as inbound voice understanding available with Ledger and Manager. Voice is converted to text/intent and used for normal Smart Business interactions.

### Malayalam Voice Reply Assistant

An older paid add-on concept focused mainly on outbound Malayalam audio confirmations using a TTS provider.

### Smart Voice Assistant Plus

A later/premium concept for deeper voice interaction, including:

- multi-turn conversations;
- longer or deeper Ask CFO analysis;
- follow-up context;
- richer explanation;
- professional text summary after voice discussion;
- action items;
- reminder continuation;
- deeper hands-free operational conversation.

Current Founder/Product Truth reconciliation already established:

- **Basic Voice is included in Ledger and Manager, particularly for Ask CFO and core input.**
- **Voice Plus is the premium depth layer.**

The older rule that outbound voice as a whole requires an add-on is superseded where it conflicts with current Product Truth.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

## 7. Voice UX and attention design

Several durable voice principles are strongly recovered:

- keep routine voice confirmations short;
- do not verbally read long tables or dense numeric reports;
- pair important audio with a concise text companion where the exact figures matter;
- allow interruption and continuation;
- permit replay through the channel where supported;
- use text rather than audio for sensitive information in public counter environments;
- use natural Kerala business language rather than formal broadcast Malayalam;
- avoid filler and robotic phrasing;
- use calm, respectful wording;
- when an error occurs, explain what happened and provide the shortest recovery path.

Ground Zero proposed a specific 12–15 second limit. That is useful UX provenance but not a constitutional constant. Current product intent is better expressed as:

> Premium does not mean longer. Premium means more useful.

Longer audio is justified only when the user wants or needs deeper conversational analysis.

## 8. Voice privacy and role boundaries

The voice layer must respect the same permission engine as text and dashboard interactions.

- owner: full permitted voice input and permitted intelligence;
- employee: only permission-scoped operational input/output;
- supplier/customer/delivery roles: only information needed for their workflow;
- sensitive owner financial intelligence must not be spoken aloud to staff by default;
- account, billing, detailed financial, employee-sensitive or security-sensitive data may require private text/dashboard presentation rather than audio.

Historical role-specific hard blocks should be converted into current permission-scoped controls rather than one rigid role list.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

# Part E — Reorder Intelligence

## 14. Reorder Intelligence — complete recommendation workflow

The strongest recovered modern workflow is:

1. **Detect need**
   - current quantity reaches a configured threshold;
   - sales velocity suggests an approaching stockout;
   - season/event patterns may affect demand where evidence exists;
   - pending purchase orders and supplier lead times are considered.

2. **Calculate a recommendation**
   - required quantity;
   - current stock;
   - target stock/runway;
   - recent sales velocity;
   - supplier lead time;
   - case/pack size where relevant.

3. **Evaluate supply options**
   - preferred supplier;
   - alternative supplier where known;
   - historical price;
   - latest known price;
   - delivery reliability/lead time;
   - recent price changes.

4. **Present owner/permitted-manager decision**
   - approve;
   - edit quantity;
   - change supplier;
   - defer/remind later;
   - reject/cancel.

5. **After approval**
   - create purchase-order record;
   - send through authorised supplier channel;
   - record dispatch status;
   - set expected delivery/follow-up;
   - prevent duplicate dispatch;
   - later match delivered invoice/stock against the order.

6. **On receipt**
   - interpret invoice/delivery document;
   - compare ordered vs delivered quantity/price;
   - ask for clarification on material mismatch;
   - update inventory after confirmation/validated workflow;
   - preserve order/invoice history.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT`

## 15. Sales-velocity calculation provenance

Ground Zero proposed a rolling 14-day model and a formula based on daily velocity, supplier lead time and safety buffer.

This is useful implementation inspiration but not a locked universal formula. Different merchant categories may need different demand windows, seasonality and reorder logic.

The enduring promise is:

> Reorder Intelligence should use the best available business evidence to make a useful recommendation and clearly show enough reasoning for the owner to decide.

**Classification:** `HISTORICAL IMPLEMENTATION DETAIL`

## 16. Owner-delegated automatic reorder

This section confirms the important evolution from unconditional automation to delegated authority.

### Historical form

Early variants ranged from simple low-stock alerts to direct automatic supplier dispatch without per-order approval.

### Current safe form

Default:

- Smart Business detects;
- prepares recommendation;
- asks owner/permitted authority to approve.

Optional standing rule:

> `When Parle-G reaches 8 packets, order 50 from Supplier X.`

A standing rule may allow automatic execution because the owner has already decided the bounded conditions.

A mature standing-rule model should preserve:

- exact item/SKU;
- threshold;
- reorder quantity or calculation policy;
- chosen supplier/approved alternatives;
- maximum price/variance boundary if used;
- channel;
- business/workspace scope;
- creator identity;
- created/modified time;
- enabled/paused/revoked state;
- trigger history;
- dispatch history;
- duplicate protection;
- failure state;
- explicit audit trail.

Only authorized owner/delegate users should create, modify, pause or revoke standing rules.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

## 17. Reorder exception handling

Recovered exception cases include:

- supplier missing/unreachable;
- supplier opted out of automated messages;
- API/channel failure;
- duplicate trigger;
- price change;
- item unavailable;
- quantity change;
- partial delivery;
- delayed delivery;
- mismatch between order and invoice;
- mismatch between ordered and received quantity;
- stale stock data;
- POS outage.

The correct doctrine is:

> Block the unsafe reorder/dispatch path, not unrelated product operation.

The owner should receive a concise explanation and recovery option.

## 25. Complete current capability family

The enduring intelligence should support objective awareness of:

- voided/cancelled bills;
- deleted/reversed transactions where available;
- unusual or policy-exceeding discounts;
- refunds/returns where available;
- cash mismatches;
- payment-mode mismatch;
- duplicate or missing bill sequence indicators where meaningful;
- unusual counter patterns;
- closing reconciliation differences;
- supporting evidence/reference;
- contextual explanation from authorized humans;
- owner review and resolution.

This is **business operational intelligence**, not automatically a security incident and not an employee verdict.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

## 26. Humanized evolution — critical correction

Ground Zero preserves strong older language such as fraud, theft, skimming, anti-cheat and automatic interception.

Current Smart Business must not say:

- `Fraud detected`;
- `Employee is stealing`;
- `Cashier is guilty`;
- automatic firing/punishment recommendations based only on system inference.

Preferred current framing:

- `A bill was cancelled at Counter 2 at 4:15 PM.`
- `A discount above your configured review level was recorded.`
- `Closing cash differs from recorded cash by ₹X.`
- `Please review this counter activity.`

The system may preserve evidence and surface the event. The owner supplies context and decides what it means.

**Classification:** `SUPERSEDED / REJECTED BEHAVIOUR` for accusation/judgement; `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT` for factual operational awareness.

## 27. Human Context Override / explanation

Section 4 strengthens the case for an explanation/context mechanism.

Example:

- system observes a bill cancel/reissue sequence;
- owner explains the card machine timed out and the bill was correctly regenerated;
- Smart Business stores both the original event and approved human context;
- future Daily Intelligence / Ask CFO reasoning can distinguish unresolved anomaly from explained event.

Do not erase original event history merely because context was added.

This is consistent with the wider Human Context Override doctrine.

# Part I — Closing Cash / End-of-Day Reconciliation

## 28. Complete workflow

The reconstructed intent is an end-of-day comparison between available business records and the merchant's real closing position.

Potential evidence sources include:

- POS sales;
- manual/voice/text Ledger entries;
- receipt/document entries;
- payment verification/bank records;
- cash/UPI/card/credit payment-mode splits;
- void/cancel/refund events;
- credit repayments;
- owner/cashier closing cash entry;
- counter/shift closing totals where integrated.

A current workflow should look like:

1. collect/synchronize available day's data;
2. identify data freshness and missing sources;
3. request physical cash/counter close value where needed;
4. compare expected vs reported/observed totals;
5. identify variance without assigning blame;
6. surface relevant bill/payment events that may explain the variance;
7. allow owner/permitted manager to add context/correction;
8. preserve reconciliation result and context;
9. feed verified/qualified information to Night Closing Intelligence and future Ask CFO analysis.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT`

## 29. Missing data behaviour

If POS or bank/payment data is missing, Smart Business must not manufacture a mismatch.

It should say what is known and what is unavailable, for example:

- Ledger entries are complete;
- POS closing file has not synced;
- bank verification is pending;
- physical drawer amount has not been entered.

Then it should offer the shortest completion path.

A missing source may make a reconciliation `incomplete`, `pending`, or `unable to verify` rather than `mismatch`.

**Classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

## 30. Mismatch is evidence, not guilt

The historical commercial problem was cash leakage. The modern design must distinguish:

- mathematical difference;
- data-sync failure;
- legitimate refund/cancel/reissue;
- delayed bank settlement;
- cash handling error;
- missing entry;
- operational exception;
- possible unexplained loss.

Only a human with business context should decide the conclusion.
