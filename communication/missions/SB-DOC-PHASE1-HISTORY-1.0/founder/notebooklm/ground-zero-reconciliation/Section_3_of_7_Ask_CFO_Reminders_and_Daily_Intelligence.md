# Part A — Ask CFO

## 2. Recovered Founder intent

Ask CFO was never intended to be a static report page or a generic chatbot.

The enduring intent is a conversational **business thinking partner** that lets the owner ask natural questions about their own business, hear or read the answer in language they naturally use, drill down through follow-up questions, understand what is fact versus interpretation, and convert useful insights into owner-approved next steps.

The strongest historical examples include questions such as:

- `Show me today's cash summary.`
- `Innathe varavum chelavum ethra roopa ayi?`
- `Show me my January entries.`
- `How much cash spent on fuel last week?`
- `Show me top supplier payments this week.`
- `Anil-nte kadam kanakkuka kaani.`
- `Show Anil credit history.`
- `Which supplier is squeezing my margins this week?`
- `Which supplier is late on delivering my low-stock bakery items?`
- `Can I pay Ravi Supplier ₹15,000 tomorrow without my Cash In Hand dipping too low?`

Ground Zero also preserves true multi-turn intent:

1. Owner: `How much did I pay for milk stock yesterday?`
2. Ask CFO answers with the relevant total and supplier context.
3. Owner: `Was that higher than the previous week?`
4. Ask CFO understands that `that` refers to the prior milk-stock question and continues the analysis without forcing the owner to restate the whole query.

**Reconciliation classification:** `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT` for conversational, contextual, owner-facing analytical dialogue.

## 4. Ask CFO analytical separation

One of the strongest useful discoveries in this section is the historical distinction between different classes of answer.

The current feature should preserve this clarity, without the exaggerated historical wording.

### 4.1 Fact

A fact is directly supported by authorised stored data.

Example:

> Your recorded expenses this week total ₹48,250.

### 4.2 Estimate

An estimate is calculated from incomplete, forward-looking, or modelled information.

Example:

> Based on your recent milk purchase pattern, next week's purchase may be around ₹15,400. This is an estimate, not a confirmed expense.

### 4.3 Pattern

A pattern describes repeated or comparative behaviour in historical data.

Example:

> Your Saturday closing differences have been higher than your weekday average during the last four weeks.

A pattern must not be converted into an accusation or unsupported causal claim.

### 4.4 Risk / thing to review

A risk is an observed condition that deserves attention.

Example:

> One customer has a high outstanding credit balance compared with the limit you set. You may want to review it before giving more credit.

### 4.5 Opportunity

An opportunity is a data-grounded possibility, not a guarantee.

Example:

> Your cash position is stronger than usual this week, and one supplier payment is due tomorrow. You may want to review whether paying it now helps your purchasing cycle.

### 4.6 Recommendation

A recommendation is advice requiring human judgement.

Example:

> I suggest reviewing the three slow-moving items before the next reorder.

The response should always make it easy for the owner to understand which statements are facts and which are interpretation.

**Reconciliation classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`.

Historical fear-heavy labels such as `HIGH-URGENCY LOSS PREVENTION RISK`, claims of theft, or aggressive upsell language are not current tone authority.

## 5. Missing, conflicting, stale, or absent data

The strongest enduring rule recovered is:

> **Ask CFO must never manufacture certainty.**

### Incomplete data

If a relevant fact exists but a field is missing, Ask CFO should state what is known and what is missing.

Example:

> I found a ₹12,000 payment to Ravi Supplier on Tuesday, but the purpose was not recorded. Tell me what it was for if you want to complete the record.

### Conflicting identity/data

If two valid interpretations exist, Ask CFO should ask the owner to choose.

Example:

> I found two Akhil profiles — one employee and one customer. Which one do you mean?

### Stale data

If a source has not refreshed, show the last known timestamp rather than presenting it as current.

Example:

> Your last verified bank update is from 11:30 PM yesterday. Newer bank activity may not be reflected yet.

### No data

A new merchant must never receive fabricated analytics.

Example:

> I don't have enough history yet to compare last month. Once you start recording transactions, I can build that view for you.

This directly supports the later Smart Business Planning correction that Ask CFO readiness must depend on actual authorised data/history rather than an arbitrary number of elapsed days.

**Reconciliation classification:** `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT`.

## 6. Voice, follow-up, summaries, and action continuation

Ground Zero preserves a valuable five-stage interaction concept:

1. natural voice question;
2. secure retrieval/reasoning;
3. concise spoken/text summary;
4. multi-turn follow-up;
5. owner-approved continuation into reminders or another feature workflow.

The durable UX intent is strong:

- owner can ask Ask CFO by text or voice;
- answer should be concise enough for a busy shop floor;
- detailed text can accompany short voice output;
- follow-up should retain conversational context;
- the owner should be able to turn an insight into a reminder/action without re-entering all context.

Example:

> Ask CFO: `Anil has ₹1,950 outstanding.`  
> Suggested next step: `Remind me to call him tomorrow.`

### Important current boundary

Ask CFO itself remains **read-only intelligence**.

The safe current interpretation is:

**Ask CFO insight → owner explicitly chooses/asks for an action → the authorised Reminder/Automation/Order/etc. engine performs the action under its own permissions and confirmation rules.**

Do not implement Ask CFO as a hidden write-capable financial agent.

## 7. Ask CFO autonomous-action prohibitions

The historical evidence and current governance strongly converge here.

Ask CFO must never autonomously:

- make final financial decisions;
- pay suppliers;
- move money;
- change credit limits merely because it thinks the change is wise;
- punish employees;
- deduct wages as a judgement;
- accuse an employee/customer of theft or fraud;
- mutate the database through generated SQL;
- delete business history;
- rewrite material financial history without the governed correction path;
- resolve ambiguous intent by guessing;
- override permissions;
- reveal owner intelligence to employees by default;
- create authority that the owner did not grant.

Current Source 05/11 rules should dominate historical implementation assumptions: Ask CFO is read-only, explains, suggests, and preserves owner decision ownership.

## 12. Reminder completion and financial actions

Historical Reminder designs sometimes combined confirmation buttons with direct financial writes, e.g.:

`Paid & Log Expense`

This is useful Founder-origin UX intent because it reduces repeated work, but it must be reconciled with financial integrity.

Current safe behaviour should be:

1. reminder contains known supplier obligation context;
2. owner explicitly confirms `Paid & Log Expense` or equivalent;
3. system presents/uses the known amount and party;
4. financial transaction is created through the normal governed Ledger transaction service;
5. confirmation reflects actual stored state;
6. reminder becomes completed;
7. both reminder history and financial auditability remain intact.

The Reminder Engine must not silently infer that a payment happened merely because the due date passed.

**Reconciliation classification:** `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`.

# Part C — Reminder vs. Automation

## 14. Correct boundary

Historical evidence repeatedly identifies the core distinction:

### Reminder

A reminder brings something to a human's attention and expects human action, acknowledgement, completion, postponement, or decision.

### Automation rule

An automation rule performs an action automatically **only because the required authority already exists**.

The current Founder-approved interpretation is stronger than the older Ground Zero `autonomous daemon` framing:

> **Automation is delegated authority, not machine sovereignty.**

Smart Business does not gain authority because a trigger exists.

---

## 15. Owner-delegated automation

Current governing model:

**Default: ask confirmation.**

The owner may explicitly create a standing rule, for example:

> When ParleG reaches 8 packets, order 50 from Moorthy Supplies.

Once stored as an authorised rule, the system may execute that specific action within the scope/limits set by the owner.

This is not the same as AI deciding by itself that ordering is wise.

### Good candidates for automatic execution after valid prior authority

- scheduled delivery of already-approved reminders/briefings;
- safe bank/payment evidence ingestion and matching;
- owner-configured stock/reorder rule execution within defined limits;
- routine system data synchronisation;
- permitted attendance capture/validation;
- notification delivery under an approved workflow;
- support FAQ responses;
- retry/idempotency/system-maintenance actions that do not redefine business decisions.

### Must still require human decision/confirmation unless separately and explicitly delegated

- new supplier purchase decision with no standing rule;
- changing customer credit limits;
- paying money or initiating a financial transfer;
- changing payroll outcome;
- approving attendance corrections;
- changing business policy;
- destructive deletion;
- resolving ambiguous business meaning;
- acting on a risk/recommendation merely because AI produced it.

### Historical behaviours specifically superseded

- hard Udhar blocking as a general business decision;
- automatic supplier ordering without owner authority;
- automatic staff punishment;
- automatic fraud/theft conclusions;
- aggressive account-data purge without the current humane lifecycle/governance path.

# Part D — Daily Intelligence Rhythm

## 16. Feature identity

Daily Intelligence is the proactive half of Smart Business intelligence.

Ask CFO is mainly **owner asks → Smart Business explains**.

Daily Intelligence is **Smart Business notices what is worth surfacing → gives the owner a calm, useful briefing without being asked**.

It should connect the business memory, reminders, credit, stock, supplier/order activity, POS/operational signals, and closing information into a predictable daily rhythm.

It must not become notification spam or an AI supervisor.

---

## 17. ACTIVE cadence — Founder approved

### 7:00 AM — Morning Business Briefing

Purpose: prepare the owner for the day with clarity.

Recovered content candidates include, when authorised and applicable:

- opening/current business position based on available data;
- previous-day income/expense summary;
- outstanding customer credit awareness;
- overnight bank/payment verification state;
- today's important reminders;
- supplier payment/follow-up due today;
- compliance dates requiring attention;
- low-stock/expiry items requiring attention;
- important order/delivery carryovers from yesterday;
- one or two high-value items to review;
- easy continuation into Ask CFO.

A Ledger merchant should receive Ledger-relevant information only. Manager can receive the deeper operational layers available in Manager.

### 10:30 AM — Business Pulse Check

Purpose: give the owner a practical mid-morning operational pulse after Kerala retail activity has started.

This Founder-approved timing supersedes the historical `09:15 AM workforce brief`.

The Pulse Check should not become an HR-only message. It can include the most relevant active operational signals such as:

- business activity so far;
- reminders still due soon;
- significant credit/payment events;
- stock issues requiring action;
- supplier/reorder status;
- important staff/attendance facts if Staff/HR is enabled;
- order/delivery progress if enabled;
- POS/operational issues for Manager if meaningful;
- only exceptions or changes that deserve owner attention.

The exact payload should be feature-aware, permission-aware, and relevance-ranked.

### 10:00 PM — Night Closing Intelligence

Purpose: help the owner close the day with confidence and reduced mental load.

Recovered content candidates include:

- day's income/expense position;
- credit issued and repayments recovered;
- pending or unresolved items;
- supplier/order state;
- reminders still pending;
- important stock/expiry issues for tomorrow;
- delivery/COD status where enabled;
- operational alerts requiring later review;
- Manager closing-cash awareness and POS intelligence where available;
- clear indication when required data is missing rather than false reconciliation.

Manager may receive the deeper closing cash/POS/operational analysis that Ledger cannot produce without those data sources.

### Add-ons remain add-ons

**Do not use Daily Intelligence as an aggressive upgrade advertisement.** The historical forced upsell messages are not current experience authority.

## 19. What deserves highlighting

Daily Intelligence should highlight information because it is materially useful, not because the system can detect it.

Examples:

- meaningful closing cash mismatch;
- important payment mismatch;
- overdue/high credit exposure;
- urgent compliance deadline;
- stockout/expiry risk;
- unresolved supplier/order problem;
- significant POS void/discount/counter activity requiring review;
- important attendance/operational exception where enabled;
- delayed delivery/COD/proof issue where enabled;
- system failure that affects the reliability of a business conclusion.

A signal should state what happened without assigning blame.

Current preferred language is respectful review language such as:

> Please review this counter activity.

not historical conclusions such as:

> Theft detected / cashier skimming attempt.

## 20. Notification-fatigue controls

High-value historical ideas worth preserving include:

- consolidate routine information into the three predictable briefings;
- deduplicate repeated events;
- do not alert repeatedly once the owner snoozes/completes an item;
- reserve interruptive alerts for genuinely time-sensitive conditions;
- use quiet text/badges for normal information;
- aggregate minor system errors for admin review rather than disturbing the merchant;
- apply humane context to attendance and operational signals;
- do not manufacture urgency from trivial variations;
- allow opt-out/unsubscribe where legally or operationally appropriate for customer/supplier communications.

The current product should prefer **calm relevance** over the historical fear/high-dopamine language.

## 22. Failure and incomplete-data handling

Historical sources contain a useful principle despite outdated implementation specifics:

> **A failed intelligence computation must not become a false business conclusion.**

### Message-generation/system failure

- log the technical failure;
- retry through the approved reliable job mechanism;
- do not claim the briefing was delivered when it was not;
- keep unrelated business functions operating;
- if needed, send a simple truthful fallback rather than fabricated analytics.

### Missing operational data

Example: POS closing data is unavailable.

Correct:

> Your ledger entries are recorded, but the POS closing data has not synced yet, so I cannot confirm the cash difference.

Incorrect:

> Cash is balanced.

### Merchant inactivity

Historical Ground Zero proposed psychologically aggressive reactivation messages after zero activity.

Current reconciliation should be gentler:

- do not fabricate activity;
- do not shame the merchant;
- if useful, explain that there is not enough recent activity for a meaningful pulse;
- offer a quick action such as recording today's total or uploading a notebook page;
- respect holidays, closures, and real human schedules.

### Historical automatic 180-day deletion

This is **not current Product Truth** and must not be revived from Ground Zero. Current subscription/data lifecycle governance requires notification, export opportunity, grace period, and humane controlled deletion handling.
