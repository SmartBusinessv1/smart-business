---

# 2. Mandatory five-bucket model

Every recovered behaviour in the seven Founder-origin sections is assigned to exactly one current reconciliation bucket:

## `PRESERVE — STILL CURRENT`

The recovered behaviour remains current product truth or directly aligned current Founder intent.

## `PRESERVE + EVOLVE`

The merchant problem, capability or workflow survives, but old authority, implementation, packaging, UX or safety behaviour must evolve to current Product Truth.

## `HISTORICAL IMPLEMENTATION DETAIL`

Useful engineering/product-history provenance that does not define present product behaviour by itself.

## `SUPERSEDED / REJECTED BEHAVIOUR`

A historical behaviour explicitly conflicts with current Founder direction, current Product Truth, human dignity, privacy, security or current architecture principles.

## `UNRESOLVED FOUNDER DECISION`

Current authoritative sources do not safely settle the product/commercial decision. The system must preserve the question rather than guess.

---

# 3. Reconciliation principles

The final pass protects against two opposite failures:

1. **Feature loss:** a Founder-created capability must not disappear because an old assistant called it `future`, `MVP later`, `after pilot`, `too complex`, `optional` or `add-on`.
2. **Unsafe revival:** an old implementation or autonomous behaviour must not return merely because it appeared in Ground Zero or V2.2 history.

Operating rule:

> **Preserve the problem. Preserve the approved capability. Preserve the Founder correction. Evolve the mechanism. Reject only the superseded behaviour.**

`Add-on` is a commercial classification, not automatically a Build Later instruction.

A narrow security or dependency blocker may stop an implementation path, but it does not silently cancel approved product commitment.
