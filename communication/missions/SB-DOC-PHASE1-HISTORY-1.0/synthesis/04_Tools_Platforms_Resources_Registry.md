## 1. Registry interpretation

- historical introduction;
- demonstrated capability;
- authorized use;
- current role;
- historical role;
- limits/authority.

Tool availability, account ownership, connector access, repository visibility and mission authority are separate facts.

## 3. What Team LIPS gained that it did not have earlier

By the end of the reconstructed period, Team LIPS could:

- coordinate multiple AI/specialist roles under one authority system;
- operate protected GitHub/PR/CI workflows;
- build with Lovable without making Lovable the source of Product Truth;
- design and verify Supabase multi-tenant RLS/data integrity;
- distinguish canonical, delivery and runtime state;
- perform independent security/permission verification;
- use AWS IAM/OIDC/Lambda with least-privilege boundaries;
- reconstruct fragmented Founder intent into mature product contracts;
- maintain program-level anti-drift registers;
- separate historical provenance from current authority.

## 4. Platform-state lessons

- `canonical repository` ≠ `delivery repository` ≠ `deployed runtime`.
- `Lovable project exists` ≠ `published` ≠ `custom domain bound` ≠ `canonical parity`.
- `Supabase project healthy` ≠ `schema deployed` ≠ `production` ≠ `application bound`.
- `AI/model can do something` ≠ `mission authorizes it`.
- `AWS account/admin capability` ≠ `deployment identity` ≠ `runtime identity`.
- `builder-side implementation` ≠ `canonical completion`.
