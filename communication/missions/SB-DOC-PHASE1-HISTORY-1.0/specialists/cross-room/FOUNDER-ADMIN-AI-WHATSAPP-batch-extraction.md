- Founder approval unless the transcript explicitly records it;
- Mission Control acceptance unless explicitly recorded;
- repository truth unless corroborated by repository evidence;
- runtime truth unless runtime evidence is shown;
- current governance authority.

Historical references to older source names, authority chains, phase labels, or room identities are preserved as historical truth. Current governance remains authoritative.

## Evidence labels

- `STRONGLY SUPPORTED` — strong evidence with a remaining naming, scope, or acceptance uncertainty.
- `CHAT-ONLY HISTORICAL` — direct historical room evidence not yet independently corroborated.

## 4.4 SB-P-1.11 Stage 10 AI & WhatsApp architecture review

### Direct historical evidence

On 4 August, Mission Control authorized a narrow `SB-P-1.11` Stage 10 EIS specialist review.

`communication/live/report1.10-ai-whatsapp.md`

Historical completion report records PR `#48` and exactly one changed file.

## 4.5 SB-P-1.11 EIS refinement verification

The room later verified the refined EIS and recorded PR `#55` with one changed file:

`communication/live/report1.12-ai-whatsapp.md`

It found many original issues resolved but retained three high-risk issues:

1. confirmation-webhook and command-idempotency binding;
2. undefined alternate confirmer / authority-transfer path;
3. incorrect use of `UNKNOWN_OUTCOME` for failures occurring before authoritative command dispatch.

The specialist again recommended no EIS lock and no implementation authority.

### Institutional lessons

#### Same-action binding

A confirmation must approve the exact action the user reviewed, not merely a semantically similar later state.

#### Permission at execution time

Conversational authorization cannot rely only on earlier UI/preview state. Current server-side permission must be revalidated when the action executes.

#### Idempotency across the whole confirmation lifecycle

Idempotency must bind the pending action, confirmation event, retries, reconciliation, and final outcome—not merely the original inbound message.

#### Authority transfer must be explicit

A user's ability to perform an action does not automatically authorize that user to confirm another user's reviewed pending action.

#### UNKNOWN_OUTCOME must mean possible authoritative write uncertainty

Failures before command dispatch are ordinary processing failures. `UNKNOWN_OUTCOME` belongs only where a command may have been committed but the caller cannot prove the result.

#### AI extraction requires provenance

Prices, taxes, costs, units, identifiers, links, and other consequential fields must preserve provenance and uncertainty. AI must not invent authoritative business facts.

#### Durable text remains important

Voice may assist the Owner, but consequential previews/results require a durable text representation.

# 7. Lessons learned register

## L1 — Project phase completion and Product Mission completion are different concepts

Phase 0 could be complete while the later SB-P mission register was still evolving. Historical reconstruction must preserve both layers distinctly.

Classification: `Project-specific → organization-wide continuity lesson`

## L2 — Estimates are not evidence

A time estimate is planning context only. Do not backfill actual mission dates or completion from estimates.

Classification: `Project-specific`

## L3 — Roadmap numbering and executed Product Mission numbering can diverge

Later retrospective summaries may conflate them. Original mission authorization + repository evidence should prevail.

Classification: `Smart Business governance/continuity lesson`

## L4 — Separate human repository orientation from AI implementation instructions

README and AGENTS serve different audiences and should not become duplicate governance documents.

Classification: `Organization-wide capability`

## L5 — Repository guidance should be durable; mission prompts should remain temporary

This early SB-P1.3 lesson directly anticipates mature repository-first AI operations.

Classification: `Organization-wide capability`

## L6 — Capability must remain dormant until the correct phase/mission authorizes it

The AI & WhatsApp room existed before AI/WhatsApp build authority and correctly remained on standby.

Classification: `Organization-wide capability`

## L7 — Calibration is not implementation

Scenario understanding can validate model/room alignment but cannot be claimed as runtime/product completion.

Classification: `Smart Business governance lesson`

## L8 — Consequential AI actions need exact confirmation contracts

Preview, actor, business, permissions, payload, target state, expiry, and idempotency must bind to the action being approved.

Classification: `Organization-wide conversational architecture capability`

## L9 — Authority transfer cannot be implied

Being permitted to perform an operation does not automatically permit confirming another user's pending action.

Classification: `Organization-wide security/permissions capability`

## L10 — Failure classes must reflect where authoritative state may have changed

Pre-dispatch AI/media errors differ from post-dispatch uncertain commit outcomes.

Classification: `Organization-wide reliability capability`
