# 3. Locked Founder Decisions Carried Without Reinterpretation

## FPDR-1 — Consequential natural-language action

Voice must reach the same governed deterministic command path as text for transaction capabilities available at that stage:

**interpretation → smallest necessary clarification → preview → confirmation binding → execution-time authorization revalidation → deterministic command → authoritative stored-state confirmation → audit/idempotency**

Voice does not receive an alternative execution path.

## FPDR-2 — Basic Voice sequencing

SB-P-1.13 owns:

- Basic Voice infrastructure;
- the governed Voice action path;
- verification against transaction capabilities actually available at that stage.

Mature credit/repayment persistence and final acceptance dependent on the four-state Ledger remain dependent on SB-P-1.14.

Affected rows remain `IN SCOPE`; they are dependency-blocked rather than reassigned.

## FPDR-3 — Raw Voice audio

Until SB-P-1.14 provides governed durable media:

- raw Voice audio is transient inside Smart Business;
- it is not durable Smart Business media;
- transcript, structured interaction/result and required audit/provenance may persist under approved policy;
- no alternate unmanaged audio-storage path is authorized.

This decision governs Smart Business retention. It does not prove anything about an external provider's retention.

## FPDR-4 — Sensitive spoken output

Authorized sensitive information must not be spoken when Smart Business already has sufficient trustworthy context that spoken disclosure is inappropriate.

Fallback is authenticated text/visual presentation.

Permission does not change.

No ambient surveillance, hidden sensing or inferred bystander detection was approved.

## FPDR-5 — Credit boundary

SB-P-1.13 may truthfully record a normal existing `sale` with `payment_method=credit`.

It may not manufacture:

- customer outstanding balance;
- mature owed-state truth;
- repayment allocation;
- four-state Ledger truth.

Those remain SB-P-1.14.

# 4. Executive Conclusion

## 4.1 Overall finding

The SB-P-1.13 Basic Voice architecture is **technically feasible and architecturally sound in principle**, provided Stage 7 Engineering and later EIS work preserve the existing single-kernel design.

The canonical design has the correct fundamental shape:

**Voice is a modality. It is not an authority system, business-memory system, second AI brain or second command path.**

The required convergence is:

**voice capture  
→ transient audio transport  
→ transcription  
→ shared language/intent layer  
→ minimum necessary clarification  
→ canonical structured proposal  
→ preview  
→ bound confirmation  
→ execution-time SB-P-1.12 authorization revalidation  
→ deterministic governed domain command  
→ authoritative stored-state outcome  
→ optional privacy-safe spoken result**

That sequence is compatible with:

- Lighthouse's human-ownership principles;
- FPDR-1 through FPDR-5;
- the locked SB-P-1.12 authority architecture;
- Contract 12;
- Contracts 9, 10, 22 and 24;
- channel independence;
- later WhatsApp reuse;
- provider replaceability.

## 4.2 What is not yet proven

Stage 7 cannot certify the following because the required runtime/provider evidence does not exist:

- actual Voice provider suitability;
- provider-side audio non-retention;
- provider-side transcript retention controls;
- provider training/improvement usage;
- actual regional processing;
- Malayalam speech accuracy;
- Manglish accuracy;
- Malayalam-English code-switch accuracy;
- Kerala-accent robustness;
- noisy-shop robustness;
- amount/name/date accuracy;
- TTS naturalness;
- production latency;

- account/project configuration;
- credential custody;

These are not reasons to replace the architecture.

They are **activation and acceptance holds**.

The most important engineering refinements are:

1. make FPDR-3 technically enforceable across every accidental persistence surface, not merely object storage;
2. make provider-side retention a separate hard evidence gate;
3. establish a Kerala merchant speech evaluation corpus before provider activation;
4. ensure a spoken confirmation operates on the already-frozen canonical proposal rather than triggering fresh AI reinterpretation;
5. treat unknown command outcome as a first-class state;
6. prevent duplicate transactions from repeated/replayed audio and retries;
7. ensure spoken `"Recorded"`, `"Saved"` or equivalent comes only from authoritative stored-state outcome;
8. ensure streaming/partial transcripts can never execute;
9. keep FPDR-4 privacy protection free of ambient surveillance;
10. build provider disable/rollback and text fallback before activation;
11. carry the Security specialist's D12/DTX holds and the AI specialist's authority/execution constraints unchanged.

## VOICE-S7-01 — Voice must terminate at the same normalized proposal and governed command boundary as text

**Severity:** High

**Area:** Basic Voice architecture / authority / domain-command convergence

**Blueprint section(s):** §8.3, §8.4, §8.5, §8.7, §12.1, §15

**Source / FPDR references:** Contract 12 §§4, 6, 11, 16; Contract 24 §§7, 15; FPDR-1; SB-P-1.12 EIS

**Repository evidence:**  
The Blueprint explicitly defines Voice as a modality over the same language, intent, permission and confirmation path as text. Security specialist evidence confirms SB-P-1.13 must consume the SB-P-1.12 authority kernel and must not create a parallel permission path. Current runtime has neither Voice nor the governed SB-P-1.13 transaction command.

**Provider evidence state:** NOT APPLICABLE

**Finding:**  
The architecture is correct, but the EIS must define one normalized post-transcription message/proposal contract that is channel-neutral. No Voice-specific parser may directly mutate business state.

STT output may inform interpretation. It may not itself become command authority.

**Merchant consequence:**  
A merchant speaking instead of typing receives exactly the same authority boundary and business truth.

**Recommendation:**  
Define a channel-neutral normalized inbound object containing, at minimum:

- actor/principal reference from authenticated server context;
- active business from verified SB-P-1.12 context;
- source modality;
- source channel;
- language metadata;
- transcript/input content;
- provider request reference where applicable;
- client/event idempotency identity;
- attachment reference only where authorized;
- no client/model-supplied permission grant.

After that point, Voice and text use the same intent, proposal, confirmation and command services.

**Product-behavior effect:** NONE

**Engineering Review dependency:** HOLD AFFECTED FINDING for D12/DTX runtime acceptance

## VOICE-S7-02 — FPDR-3 requires a full transient-audio boundary, not merely “do not upload to object storage”

**Severity:** High

**Area:** Raw audio / privacy / retention

**Blueprint section(s):** §8.7, §8.3, §8.6, §8.11, §12.2

**Affected FCTM row IDs:**  
`12-§15-4`, `12-§16-1` through `12-§16-8`, `24-§25-1` through `24-§25-9`, `24-§31-14`, `22-§23-2`, `22-§23-3`, `22-§23-5`, `22-§23-7`

**Source / FPDR references:** FPDR-3; Contract 12 §§15–17; Contract 24 §25

**Repository evidence:**  
No Voice storage or provider exists. The current repository contains a catalog-specific transient parser path but no approved Voice media path.

**Provider evidence state:** UNVERIFIED — ACCESS REQUIRED for any external provider

**Finding:**  
“Transient raw audio” can be violated unintentionally without creating a deliberate media table or storage bucket.

Potential persistence routes include:

- browser/mobile temporary recording caches;
- server/framework request buffering;
- multipart-upload temp files;
- temporary filesystem spill;
- CDN/reverse-proxy body capture;
- request logging;
- application debug logging;
- error payload logging;
- crash dumps;
- APM payload capture;
- observability replay;
- retry queues;
- dead-letter payloads;
- provider request archives;
- test fixtures made from real merchant audio;
- backup inclusion of temp directories;
- analytics event payloads.

**Merchant consequence:**  
A merchant may reasonably believe a Voice note disappears after processing while copies actually survive in logs, buffers or diagnostics.

**Recommendation:**  
The EIS should define a formal **Transient Audio Handling Contract** requiring:

1. memory/stream processing where practical;
2. any unavoidable temp file to use non-public, process-scoped storage with deterministic deletion;
3. raw audio excluded from normal application logs;
4. raw audio excluded from error logging;
5. raw audio excluded from analytics;
6. raw audio excluded from standard observability payload capture;
7. retry logic to reference a transient request identity rather than silently creating a durable audio queue unless separately approved;
8. crash/debug modes disabled from capturing merchant audio in production;
9. no Voice object-storage write before governed durable media is available;
10. deletion of application-controlled temporary copies after terminal processing;
11. explicit test evidence for failure/interruption paths as well as successful paths.

The system may persist the approved transcript, canonical proposal/result and audit metadata where policy permits.

**Product-behavior effect:** NONE

**Engineering Review dependency:** HOLD AFFECTED FINDING until transient handling is explicitly specified

**Trigger screen:** T6 candidate

**Stage 7 acceptance implication:** FPDR-3 cannot be considered engineering-complete from “no storage bucket” alone.

**Area:** Confirmation binding / speech-specific confirmation risks

**Blueprint section(s):** §8.5, §8.7, §15

**Affected FCTM row IDs:**  
`12-§6-6`, `12-§7-1` through `12-§7-10`, `12-§17-1` through `12-§17-15`, `24-§20-1` through `24-§20-10`, `24-§21-1`, `24-§21-2`, `24-§21-8` through `24-§21-10`, `24-§31-7`, `10-§7-*`, `BP-§10.2-SA1`

**Source / FPDR references:** FPDR-1; SB-P-1.12 confirmation-binding architecture; AI specialist AI-S7-02

**Repository evidence:**  
Locked SB-P-1.12 EIS provides proposal/confirmation-binding primitives; runtime consumption by SB-P-1.13 is not yet implemented.

**Provider evidence state:** NOT APPLICABLE

**Finding:**  
Voice creates confirmation hazards absent or weaker in button-based UI:

- `"yes"` misheard from unrelated background speech;
- partial `"yes"`;
- stale `"yes"` after proposal expiry;
- double `"yes"`;
- barge-in;
- audio event replay;
- confirmation after authority state changed.

The confirmation utterance may resolve only the user's confirm/reject intent.

It must not regenerate or reinterpret the business proposal.

A confirmed proposal containing ₹500/Rahman/credit cannot become ₹5,000/Raman/cash because the model is called again.

**Merchant consequence:**  
Prevents confirmation drift and accidental financial actions.

**Recommendation:**  
For consequential Voice confirmation:

1. server canonicalizes proposal;
2. server produces fingerprint;
3. preview exposes canonical fields;
4. confirmation is bound to actor, business, proposal fingerprint and validity/authority epochs;
5. spoken `"yes"` resolves only confirmation intent;
6. execution consumes the already-stored binding;
7. any material proposal change invalidates the old binding;
8. stale/duplicated confirmation becomes no-op/rejected outcome;
9. execution-time authorization is revalidated.

For high-risk ambiguity in the confirmation audio itself, prefer explicit re-prompt or a deterministic tap control rather than guessing.

**Product-behavior effect:** NONE

**Engineering Review dependency:** HOLD AFFECTED FINDING until SB-P-1.12 binding and DTX command are implemented and consumed

# 14. Final Specialist Finding

Smart Business can realistically provide useful Basic Voice to Kerala merchants without creating a separate Voice authority system.

The design remains consistent with the Lighthouse principles only if Voice is treated as a **convenience interface into the same human-owned business system**, not as an autonomous assistant with its own truth.

The strongest final safety boundary is therefore:

> Speech may be probabilistic.  
> Interpretation may be probabilistic.  
> Business authority and stored business truth may not be.

Smart Business should be tolerant of how naturally a merchant speaks while becoming increasingly deterministic as the request approaches a consequential business action.

That means:

**natural speech → careful interpretation → minimal clarification → explicit proposal → bound human confirmation → deterministic authority → deterministic command → authoritative result**

—not:

**natural speech → confident model guess → business write**.

This architecture is feasible.

Its provider readiness and runtime correctness are not yet proven.
