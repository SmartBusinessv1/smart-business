# MC-613 — Research & Intelligence provisional legal/privacy report

**Mission:** MC-612-B — location-specific Indian legal/privacy research  
**Date:** 29 October 2026  
**Status:** **PROVISIONAL, NON-LAWYER RESEARCH — READ ONLY**  
**Scope:** employee attendance location; delivery-proof location; customer delivery destinations.  
**Boundary:** no Product Truth change, no retention duration adopted, no FCTM edit, no implementation advice, no Git write.

## Executive conclusion

The strongest supportable position is that the three location-data classes should **not be treated as one retention class**.

Employee attendance geolocation, delivery-proof geolocation, and customer delivery destinations serve different purposes, have different legal anchors, and should have different access-expiry and storage logic. Current Indian/Kerala law provides some clear record-retention duties around employment, GST/accounting, delivery and disputes, but I found **no current Indian or Kerala rule that generally requires a merchant SaaS product to retain raw GPS coordinates for a fixed period for any of these three classes**.

The most important forward-looking constraint is the DPDP regime. As of 29 October 2026, the core private-sector processing, notice, consent/legitimate-use, security, erasure and Data Principal-right provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 are **not yet in force**. The commencement notification brings the main substantive provisions into force only after the 18-month tranche; a smaller one-year tranche also has not yet commenced as of today. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That means Phase One design should distinguish:

| Layer | Finding |
|---|---|
| Current legal duty | Existing IT Act/SPDI, labour, GST, consumer, contract/limitation and CERT-In obligations apply now where relevant. |
| Future DPDP design | Smart Business should prepare now for purpose limitation, notice, minimization, erasure and lawful-retention exceptions that will become operative with the substantive DPDP tranche. |
| Product design | Raw location should not be kept merely because the system can keep it. |
| Qualified review | Exact lawful basis, merchant/Data Fiduciary role allocation, employment notice/consent wording, and final retention periods still require professional legal/privacy validation. |

## 1. Current-law / staged-commencement position

The DPDP Act was enacted in 2023, but commencement is staged. The 13 November 2025 Gazette notification immediately commenced only the institutional/administrative provisions identified in paragraph (a); the one-year tranche covers section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d); the main substantive processing regime, including sections 3–17 and most enforcement provisions, comes into force 18 months after publication. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Accordingly, on **29 October 2026**:

| Provision group | Current position |
|---|---|
| DPDP core lawful-processing, notice, consent, employment legitimate-use, general Data Fiduciary obligations, erasure, Data Principal rights | **Not yet operative** |
| One-year tranche | **Not yet operative** |
| DPDP institutional/Board-related provisions in first tranche | Operative |
| IT Act section 43A / 2011 SPDI framework | Still materially relevant until the DPDP amendment removing/replacing that framework becomes operative |
| CERT-In 2022 logging direction | Operative |
| Labour / GST / consumer / limitation law | Operative subject to applicability |

The current IT/SPDI rules define “personal information” broadly as information capable of identifying a natural person directly or indirectly. They separately list the narrower categories treated as “sensitive personal data or information”; ordinary location data is not expressly listed among those sensitive categories, although identifiable geolocation can still be personal information. Rule 4 also requires a body corporate handling personal information to publish a privacy policy describing data types, purpose and disclosure practices. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/sites/upload_files/dit/files/RNUS_CyberLaw_15411.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 2. Employee attendance location

### Legal findings

Kerala labour materials require employers to maintain employment-related records. Kerala Labour Commissionerate guidance identifies, among other records, the **Register of Employment**, service record and leave/holiday registers. [Labour Commissionerate](https://lc.kerala.gov.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ%20under%20Kerala%20Shops%20and%20Commercial%20Establishments%20Act_1.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I found **no Kerala statutory source requiring raw GPS coordinates to be retained as part of an attendance record**.

That distinction matters. The legal requirement to maintain evidence of employment/attendance does not automatically create a legal requirement to preserve:

- latitude/longitude;
- a precise location trail;
- continuous employee movement;
- a device-location history.

The Founder direction against continuous tracking is therefore consistent with the evidence reviewed.

The DPDP Act's future substantive regime is especially relevant here because section 7 includes processing for employment purposes among the “certain legitimate uses”; this can potentially support narrowly necessary attendance processing without treating every employment-data flow as consent-dependent. The same future regime also imposes purpose-linked processing, security and erasure requirements. The substantive sections are not yet operative. [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22037/2/a2023-22.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The Supreme Court has separately treated location tracking as privacy-significant. In *Frank Vitus v. Narcotics Control Bureau*, the Court rejected a condition effectively enabling tracking of a person's movements and linked such tracking to Article 21 privacy concerns. That case is not an employment case and does not itself create a private-employer retention rule, but it strongly supports caution against continuous or disproportionate location surveillance. [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/landmark-judgment-summaries/?judgment_year=2024\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Evidence-supported design options

| Option | Description | Legal/privacy posture |
|---|---|---|
| A | Use location only momentarily to evaluate whether the employee is inside the permitted attendance geofence; store no raw coordinates | Strongest minimization position |
| B | Store attendance time + business/site ID + “inside/outside permitted area” result; discard raw coordinates after validation | Strong balance between auditability and minimization |
| C | Retain raw coordinates only for a defined exception, such as a disputed attendance event, fraud/security investigation or legal hold | Potentially defensible but needs tightly scoped access and qualified review |
| D | Retain raw coordinates routinely with attendance for a long period | **Not supported by a specific retention requirement found in this research** |

### Worker notice finding

Even before the future DPDP notice regime becomes operative, the current SPDI privacy-policy framework supports transparent disclosure of personal-information collection and purpose. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/sites/upload_files/dit/files/RNUS_CyberLaw_15411.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For Phase One, a worker-facing notice should therefore be treated as the safer design assumption: disclose that location is collected **only at the attendance event**, its purpose, whether exact coordinates are stored, who can access the result, and when access/storage ends.

**Uncertainty:** I found no Kerala labour rule that specifically prescribes the wording or consent mechanism for geofence attendance. Qualified counsel should determine whether employment-law fairness/contract, standing-order, union/industrial relations, or future DPDP requirements require additional notice/consent mechanisms for particular employer classes.

## 3. Delivery-proof location

### Legal findings

Delivery proof can support several independent business/legal purposes:

- proof that goods were delivered;
- resolution of non-delivery or delayed-delivery disputes;
- COD reconciliation;
- GST/accounting records;
- consumer complaints;
- contractual claims.

The Limitation Act gives several relevant contract/carriage claims a three-year limitation period, including carrier claims for non-delivery/delay and claims for the price of goods sold and delivered. This does **not** create a universal “retain location for three years” obligation, but it demonstrates why evidence may need preservation when a live or reasonably anticipated dispute exists. [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1565/5/A1963-36.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Consumer Affairs guidance states that a consumer complaint is ordinarily to be filed within two years from the cause of action. Again, that is a litigation/complaint window, not a statutory GPS-retention command. [Consumer Affairs](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/file-uploads/latestnews/FAQ.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

GST law can independently require underlying transaction and delivery-related records. Section 36 of the CGST Act requires registered persons to retain books/accounts and relevant records for 72 months from the due date of the annual return, with longer retention where specified proceedings/investigations remain pending. [CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/CGST-Act-Updated-31082021.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com) The GST rules also require invoices, delivery challans and related delivery records to be preserved under that section. [CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/accnt-record-rules.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

But the important distinction is:

**delivery record ≠ raw geolocation coordinate.**

Nothing in the GST sources reviewed requires latitude/longitude to be stored merely because a delivery occurred.

### Evidence-supported design options

| Option | Description | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| A | After completion, preserve only a delivery event: order ID, timestamp, delivery status, authorized actor and merchant/customer confirmation where applicable | Strong minimization baseline |
| B | Preserve a coarse proof such as site/address match or a cryptographic/hash-backed proof event, without retaining raw GPS | Strong candidate where stronger evidence is needed |
| C | Retain exact point-in-time delivery coordinate only where the merchant expressly enables it for proof/dispute purposes, with restricted access and defined deletion/hold rules | Plausible, but requires later legal/privacy validation |
| D | Continuous delivery-staff route tracking or permanent route history | **Material privacy concern; not supported by the reviewed law and inconsistent with the Founder boundary** |

For COD, the financial/payment fact and the location fact should remain separate. A merchant may need to preserve the COD/payment record even after exact delivery coordinates are no longer justified.

## 4. Customer delivery destinations

### Legal findings

A customer's delivery address is directly necessary to fulfil a delivery transaction. Under GST invoicing rules, recipient and delivery addresses are required in specified circumstances; for example, Rule 46 requires the recipient name/address and delivery address in specified unregistered-recipient transactions. [CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Therefore some delivery-address data may lawfully need to survive as part of a statutory invoice or business record where GST requirements apply.

However, the **operational delivery destination** can be broader than the statutory invoice address. Examples include:

- precise map pin;
- building entrance;
- floor/door instructions;
- temporary landmark;
- live location;
- gate or pickup point.

Those extra details do not automatically inherit the retention period of the tax invoice simply because they were used during delivery.

The future DPDP regime points toward erasure when the specified purpose ends unless retention is required by law. The final DPDP Rules also include a future one-year minimum retention requirement for specified processing records/logs under Rule 8(3), subject to the Seventh Schedule and other-law exceptions; that rule belongs to the future-effective tranche and is not yet operative today. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Evidence-supported design options

| Destination element | Strongest provisional treatment |
|---|---|
| Invoice-required postal address | Retain where applicable statutory/accounting records require it |
| Customer's reusable saved address | Retain while the customer/merchant still needs it for the specified service, subject to future DPDP rights/erasure framework |
| One-time delivery address | Separate from reusable profile data; expire access once delivery/dispute need ends |
| Exact GPS/map pin | Treat as higher-sensitivity operational location data; do not automatically retain with the invoice |
| Delivery instructions / landmarks | Minimize and expire separately unless genuinely needed for a live order, complaint or claim |

## 5. Access expiry versus retention

MC-612 correctly requires these to be separated.

A record can remain lawfully stored while ordinary operational access expires. For all three location classes, a defensible architecture should distinguish at least:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Active operational access | Needed to perform attendance/delivery/order task |
| Restricted post-purpose retention | Data retained only for a defined statutory, audit, dispute or security basis |
| Legal hold | Ordinary deletion suspended because a concrete proceeding, claim, investigation or notice requires preservation |
| Erased / anonymized | No continuing lawful/product purpose supports identifiable retention |

That model is more strongly supported than treating “retained” as meaning “visible forever to normal users.”

## 6. CERT-In and security logs

CERT-In's 2022 directions require service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations to enable logs of ICT systems and retain them securely for a rolling **180 days within India**. [CERT-IN](https://cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This does **not** mean Smart Business must place employee/customer GPS data into logs. It means that if location-related activity is represented in covered ICT logs, the logging requirement may affect that logged representation.

Design implication: avoid writing unnecessary raw geolocation into broad application/security logs. A minimally necessary event identifier or action result is preferable where sufficient.

## 7. Material STOP findings

No absolute legal prohibition on point-in-time employee attendance geofencing, delivery-proof location, or delivery-destination processing was found in the authoritative sources reviewed.

However, the following **would require STOP / escalation before a provisional F-1 design decision**:

| Trigger | Why |
|---|---|
| Continuous employee tracking or route surveillance proposed | Material privacy/proportionality concern; not supported by the identified attendance-record duties |
| Exact GPS proposed as a universally retained attendance record | No statutory requirement found; minimization question unresolved |
| One retention period proposed for all three classes | Evidence shows different legal purposes and records |
| GST retention used to justify keeping all location metadata | GST supports specified business records, not every operational location field |
| Consumer/limitation periods treated as automatic retention mandates | They are claim windows/evidence considerations, not universal location-retention statutes |
| Future DPDP requirements treated as already operative | Core provisions remain staged |
| Future Rule 8 one-year processing-log rule treated as authority for a present retention policy | It is not yet operative and its exact application to Smart Business/location fields needs qualified interpretation |
| Merchant/employer role versus Team LIPS Data Fiduciary/Data Processor role remains undefined for a processing flow | Role allocation affects obligations |
| Location used for punishment, scoring or automatic adverse employee decisions | Raises heightened accuracy, fairness, employment and privacy risk beyond simple attendance verification |

## 8. Questions requiring qualified legal/privacy review

Qualified review remains necessary on: whether individual merchants or Team LIPS are Data Fiduciary/joint Data Fiduciary/Data Processor for each flow; whether future section 7 employment legitimate-use fully covers point-in-time geofence processing and what notice is still required; whether exact coordinates may be retained for attendance disputes and under what conditions; whether merchant employee contracts/standing orders require location-monitoring disclosure; how Rule 8(3)'s future one-year processing-log requirement applies to location events and processors; what precise customer-address components must remain with GST/statutory records; and how long defensible claims/legal-hold retention should persist once a specific dispute exists.

## 9. Provisional recommendation to MC-613

The evidence supports carrying **three separate F-1 product-design decisions**, not one generalized retention number:

**Employee attendance:** prefer point-in-time geofence verification with raw-coordinate minimization; retain the attendance fact rather than movement history.

**Delivery proof:** retain the delivery-proof event necessary for merchant, dispute, COD and audit purposes; treat exact location as optional, narrowly justified evidence rather than a universal record.

**Customer delivery destination:** retain statutory/order address information only to the extent required for the relevant transaction/record; separate operational map pins and delivery instructions from the long-lived customer/invoice record.

No duration should be selected from this research alone.
