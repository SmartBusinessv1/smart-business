## 1. Independent validation result

The earlier MC-612-B report is **substantially supported**, but one legal point needs narrowing.

I independently confirm that I found **no Indian central or Kerala authority requiring Smart Business or a Kerala merchant to retain raw GPS coordinates for a prescribed period merely because those coordinates were used for attendance, delivery proof, or customer delivery navigation**.

The law more clearly supports preservation of the **underlying statutory/business record**—for example an employment register, tax invoice, delivery challan, transaction record, security log, or dispute record—than preservation of the exact latitude/longitude that may have helped create or validate that record.

The important correction is this: under the current 2011 IT/SPDI Rules, Rule 4's privacy-policy requirement expressly covers **personal information**, but Rule 5's necessity, collection and retention restrictions are drafted for **sensitive personal data or information (SPDI)**. Ordinary identifiable location is capable of being “personal information,” but location is not itself listed in the Rule 3 SPDI categories. The earlier report should therefore **not** be read as saying that current Rule 5 independently imposes a general statutory location-data erasure rule. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/sites/upload_files/dit/files/RNUS_CyberLaw_15411.pdf)

The stronger general purpose-limitation/erasure framework comes from the DPDP Act and Rules, but the core provisions relevant here are **future-effective as of 29 October 2026**, not operative today. G.S.R. 843(E) places most substantive processing provisions, including sections 3–17, into the 18-month tranche from 13 November 2025; that points to **13 May 2027**. The one-year tranche does not begin until **13 November 2026**. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf)

## 2. Current-law versus staged-law position

| Area | 29 Oct 2026 position | Research consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Kerala employment registers | Current | Employer recordkeeping is real; raw GPS requirement not identified |
| GST accounts/invoices/delivery records | Current | Statutory records can require long retention; raw GPS does not automatically inherit it |
| IT Act / SPDI Rules | Current | Privacy policy/security framework applies; current general raw-location retention rule is not established |
| CERT-In logging directions | Current | ICT logs have a 180-day rule; this does **not** require creating or preserving raw GPS |
| DPDP core processing, employment legitimate-use, general erasure rights | Future — principal tranche | Strong design signal, not yet current operative rule |
| DPDP Rules 6–16, including Rule 8 | Future — 18-month tranche | Cannot presently be used as a current raw-GPS mandate |

Kerala's Labour Commissionerate states that covered employers maintain a **Register of Employment (Form A), Service Record (Form BB), holiday/leave registers and other employment records**. The source does not identify latitude/longitude as a required field. [Labour Commissionerate](https://lc.kerala.gov.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ%20under%20Kerala%20Shops%20and%20Commercial%20Establishments%20Act_1.pdf)

GST section 36 requires applicable registered persons to retain prescribed books/accounts and records for 72 months from the annual-return trigger, with longer preservation for specified proceedings. CBIC's rules also preserve invoices and delivery challans under that statutory regime. That is strong authority for the **business/tax record**, but not for raw geolocation unless raw geolocation is independently part of a legally required record—which I did not establish. [CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/CGST-bill-e.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

CERT-In requires covered entities to enable ICT-system logs and keep them for a rolling 180 days in India. Its text does not say “collect location,” nor does it turn GPS coordinates into mandatory log fields. [CERT-IN](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)

## 4. Can an event-triggered erasure rule be framed without inventing days?

**Yes, provisionally.**

A legally cautious product-design option can be framed around **events and purposes**, not arbitrary numeric periods:

> Raw precise location is available only while necessary to perform or validate the attendance, delivery or navigation event. Once that operational purpose is complete, the raw coordinate is erased unless a specifically identified statutory requirement, live dispute, investigation, security incident or legal hold requires that coordinate itself to remain. Any surviving statutory/business record is retained separately from the raw location.

That is a **product-design option**, not a declaration of current statutory law.

Its strength is that it avoids inventing a duration and keeps five concepts separate:

| Concept | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Operational access | Ends with the task or validation need |
| Raw-coordinate storage | Ends with purpose unless specifically justified |
| Statutory record | Survives according to its own law |
| Dispute/legal hold | Temporarily overrides normal erasure only for identified evidence |
| Erasure | Applies to the raw location when no continuing basis remains |

This pattern is consistent with the future DPDP architecture. Section 12 will require erasure on request unless retention remains necessary for the specified purpose or compliance with law, once that section commences. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Rule 8 of the 2025 DPDP Rules also introduces future retention/erasure mechanics, including a one-year processing/log rule for specified purposes and classes, but Rules 5–16 do **not** commence until the 18-month tranche. Therefore the rule cannot be treated as current authority for selecting a Phase One raw-GPS duration today. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

## 6. Current SPDI framework — corrected interpretation

The current 2011 Rules define personal information broadly enough to cover information capable of identifying a person. Rule 4 requires a body corporate handling personal information to publish a privacy policy that states the information collected, purpose, disclosure practices and security framework. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/sites/upload_files/dit/files/RNUS_CyberLaw_15411.pdf)

But the stronger Rule 5 requirements—necessity, collection notice and “not longer than required” retention—are drafted around **sensitive personal data or information**. The enumerated SPDI categories include passwords, financial data, health, sexual orientation, medical records and biometrics; ordinary location is not expressly enumerated. [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/sites/upload_files/dit/files/RNUS_CyberLaw_15411.pdf)

Therefore:

**Current-law claim:** location can be personal information.

**Not established:** every raw-location record is SPDI merely because it is precise.

**Not established:** current SPDI Rule 5 by itself creates a general statutory raw-location deletion deadline.

This is a material qualification for MC-614.

## 7. Material STOP findings

There is **no discovered universal prohibition** on point-in-time attendance geofencing, point-in-time delivery proof, or processing a customer destination for delivery.

That is different from saying all designs are safe.

A **material STOP / Founder re-escalation** exists if the proposed F-1 choice requires any of the following: routine continuous employee tracking; persistent route surveillance; automatic retention of exact coordinates merely because statutory attendance/GST records survive; treating CERT-In logs as a mandate to store GPS; assigning one numeric retention period to all three data classes; treating future DPDP provisions as operative today; keeping raw location indefinitely “for audit”; or using exact location for punitive/automated employment decisions beyond the already-authorized attendance purpose.

Those designs lack support from the primary authorities reviewed and raise materially greater privacy/proportionality issues.

## 9. MC-613-B source-sufficiency assessment

The evidence is sufficient to give the Founder **non-numeric, class-specific F-1 design choices**.

It is **not sufficient** to give the Founder a legally validated number of days/months/years for raw location.

For F1-R, Mission Control can truthfully say the research supports these bounded option families:

**Attendance:** no raw GPS, validation/event fact only, or precise coordinate only under a specific justified hold.
