## **3\. Approved POS adapter-class assessments**

Class A — Authenticated API / webhook

Design feasible · source unverified

Supports a vendor-neutral adapter receiving authenticated POS transaction events where the POS offers an appropriate interface.

Authority: 17:FPDR-2; Blueprint §§8.6, 20.4.6 and 20.9.3.

The EIS must define source authentication, server-side integration identity, permission scope, payload validation, stable event identity, transport replay protection, retries, ordering, corrections, credential rotation and failure recovery. Vendor signatures must be verified if applicable; signature support must not be assumed universally.

Architectural risk: webhook delivery success is not equivalent to a successfully committed Ledger transaction. Receipt, normalization and business posting require separately traceable states.

Class B — Structured export through UDI

Design feasible · source unverified

Supports POS-originated structured records through the existing Universal Document Intelligence pathway, rather than a new import engine.

Authority: 17:FPDR-2; Blueprint §§8.6, 20.4.5–20.4.6 and 20.9.1.

The EIS must specify validated schema, file provenance, encoding, source and row identities, re-export treatment, completeness indicators, preview/confirmation where applicable, failed-row handling and governed posting.

Important: A content fingerprint can contribute duplicate evidence but cannot substitute for a stable source-event identity. The approved draft explicitly preserves this distinction.

Architectural risk: daily exports can contain repeated sales, revised sales, incomplete periods or summary data. None may silently become independent revenue.

### **Comparative conclusion**

Both classes are necessary to preserve the Founder decision. Neither may be silently removed because the first merchant POS happens to support the other class.

The integration should use one normalized bridge contract and one governed posting path. The adapters may vary; Ledger, stock, permissions and audit authority must not.

An export-capable POS may qualify for normal sales ingestion without supporting Scenario B. Conversely, evidence of a discount field alone does not establish safe financial ingestion.

### **5.1 Critical finding SA04-F01 — Atomic composition**

Severity: HIGH

EQ17-13 is not a cosmetic implementation detail.

The governed Ledger command `create_transaction_governed`, specified under SB-P-1.14, and `create_inventory_movement`, from the inventory foundation, must compose into a single atomic business transaction where a command produces both effects.

Two successful API calls are not equivalent to one database transaction. If the movement commits and the financial write fails, later retries can produce discrepancies even if each operation is independently idempotent.

Proposed control: The EIS must establish an approved transactional composition mechanism, demonstrate rollback under injected failure, and show that no alternate direct-write path bypasses the governed command.

The existing `src/integrations/supabase/transactions.ts` contains a direct `transactions` insert path. This is current pre-cutover implementation evidence, not proof that the future governed `DTX` cutover has occurred. It must be included in the cutover and regression review rather than misreported as already closed.

### **5.2 Finding SA04-F02 — Event-link trust boundary**

Severity: HIGH

The inspected `tests/inventory/trusted-event-link.test.ts` explicitly documents that the existing movement foundation validates the event-type/ID pairing but does not validate whether a referenced future-domain event exists or belongs to the same business.

The test permits a well-formed, presently unvalidated event reference within its approved scope.

This is not, by itself, a defect in the inventory foundation. It is precisely why INV-A3 must be implemented by the owning POS/business-event domain before POS-linked movements are trusted.

Required negative tests include nonexistent reference, cross-business reference, wrong event type, reversed reference and unauthorized principal.

### **5.3 Finding SA04-F03 — POS/manual overlap**

Severity: HIGH

A Kerala merchant might record a sale through a POS and later enter it manually through WhatsApp or the dashboard. These are different source identities for what may be the same commercial event.

Event-ID deduplication alone cannot identify that overlap.

The approved INV-A7 design requires a review candidate rather than an automatic merge, suppression or deletion.

The EIS must settle matching rules, confidence presentation, authoritative user confirmation, correction provenance, and the way unresolved overlap affects financial and closing reports.

The particularly important distinction is that keeping both original records for evidence must not cause the reporting experience to silently present potentially duplicated revenue as verified business truth.

No autonomous duplicate judgment should replace the Owner's determination.

### **Financial-specific review concerns**

The first real POS dossier should establish how the source represents gross and net amounts, GST/tax treatment, line-level versus invoice-level discounts, returns, voided bills, partial cancellations, split payments, store credit, cash refunds, offline settlement and corrected receipts.

These are validation questions, not assertions that any specific POS supplies those fields. Where missing semantics would make a transaction ambiguous, the source must be constrained or held rather than inventing interpretation.

A review signal must also distinguish a legitimate cancelled bill from a completed sale followed by a refund. Treating both as identical can corrupt sales totals, stock and closing cash.

### **Privacy-specific review concerns**

Operator-attributed detail remains Owner-reserved under `17:FPDR-1`; ordinary Manager permissions do not confer it.

The U-11 legal/privacy hold must cover collection necessity, permitted purpose, source/vendor terms, staff notice where required, minimization, retention, export exposure and deletion handling.

A real POS source may be used for business-level Scenario B verification without requiring employee identification. That is preferable when it supplies sufficient business-level discount or void evidence.

### **7.3 Review wording**

An acceptable example, subject to actual source evidence, is:

The message must not imply that unusually high discounts establish fraud, dishonesty or misconduct.

No universal discount percentage, such as 15%, should be introduced as Product Truth. A practical threshold or comparison baseline is an engineering and evidence question, not a replacement for human decision ownership.

## **Database grants, RLS and default-deny**

The repository history makes this a material migration concern.

`20260727000000_reconcile_default_grants.sql` established broad defaults for objects created by `postgres`, including broad table rights for `authenticated`/`service_role` and function `EXECUTE`. Later migrations demonstrate the safer pattern: explicit `REVOKE` first, RLS, then narrow re-grant. The parser migration also shows why granting a narrow privilege without first removing an inherited broader one does not narrow effective privilege.

For M17, the EIS should require:

* every new table to start effectively unusable by browser roles;  
* explicit `REVOKE ALL` from **PUBLIC, anon and authenticated**, not merely `anon`;  
* explicit treatment of `service_role` rather than accepting inherited `ALL`;  
* RLS enabled before any browser-facing grant is usable;  
* no broad `USING (true)` / `WITH CHECK (true)` merchant policy;  
* Manager access through the SB-P-1.12 kernel, not new `owner_id` convenience policies;  
* explicit sequence privileges where generated sequences exist;  
* function `PUBLIC EXECUTE` neutralized before intended grants;  
* effective-ACL verification, including creator-role default privileges, rather than migration-text inspection alone.

This directly reuses the SB-P-1.12 readiness finding that **effective state, not source text, is the security evidence**.

## **SECURITY INVOKER / function security**

Blueprint §20.4.11 says new functions use `SECURITY INVOKER`, RLS and fixed `search_path`. That is a good default for ordinary M17 readers and domain operations, but the EIS should not treat the words `SECURITY INVOKER` as sufficient proof.

## **Rollback and compensating-migration conditions**

The generic §20.7.3 requirement is sound but needs class-specific treatment.

A future M17 migration package should classify each change as one of:

* **pre-data reversible:** structural addition can be removed because no accepted new data relies on it;

* **cut-over reversal:** authority path may be restored only if rehearsed and no new incompatible state has been committed;  
* **disaster recovery:** PITR/restore is a last-resort environment recovery mechanism, not normal logical rollback.

Specific examples:

* M17-1 movement-type/batch data becomes forward-fix territory once new movements exist.  
* M17-3 supplier attribution/history must never be “rolled back” by deleting historical links/data after accepted use.  
* M17-6 raw POS/source events require preservation after ingestion; fixes should be additive/corrective.  
* M17-7 historical closing results/corrections are append/history-oriented.  
* M17-9 cannot roll back into a dual-write or direct-DML state after governed effects have been created.

Every compensating migration must itself be independently reviewed and rehearsed.

# **6\. INV-A2 / EQ17-13 — atomic stock \+ Ledger commit**

## **Finding SA03-FI-02 — Atomicity wording is correct, but this remains the dominant financial hold**

**Severity:** CRITICAL/HIGH  
**Disposition:** REFINEMENT REQUIRED / EIS BLOCKING CONDITION

The intended invariant is correct:

> Ledger event \+ inventory movement(s) \+ source-event state \+ event linkage must all commit in one database transaction or none commit.

This is the correct financial design.

A sequence such as:

1. post inventory;  
2. call Ledger RPC;  
3. second call fails;

is **not acceptable**, even when both individual calls are idempotent.

Idempotency is not a substitute for atomicity.

### **Minimum acceptable EIS architecture**

There must be one transaction-owning composite command for each event that requires both domains.

That transaction must:

1. establish source-event/request idempotency;  
2. lock/revalidate source state;  
3. authorize the actor/principal;  
4. create the SB-P-1.14 Ledger effect through its governed interface;  
5. create every required inventory movement;  
6. validate links;  
7. write source-event outcome/provenance;  
8. commit all effects together;  
9. store one committed command outcome.

If any sub-step fails, **everything rolls back**.

### **What is not sufficient**

* two HTTP/RPC calls with compensating cleanup;  
* “eventual consistency” between stock and Ledger;  
* committing stock first and retrying finance later;  
* committing finance first and retrying stock later;  
* independent idempotency keys with no common committed outcome;  
* background reconciliation presented as equivalent to atomic commit.

### **Ownership constraint**

The composite transaction must preserve domain ownership:

* SB-P-1.14 remains owner of Ledger semantics;  
* inventory command remains owner of movement semantics;  
* SB-P-1.17 owns orchestration/business-event linkage.

If the currently locked command interfaces cannot be safely composed within one database transaction, **EQ17-13 remains unresolved and the dependent EIS path must stop**.

If engineering proves composition impossible without changing a locked upstream command contract, Mission Control must determine whether an upstream amendment/T8 screen is required.

At present, infeasibility has **not** been demonstrated.

# **13\. INV-A9 — supplier receipt versus payment**

## **Finding SA03-FI-07 — Supplier receipt/payment distinction is correct; “Ledger expense” needs precision**

**Severity:** HIGH  
**Disposition:** REFINEMENT REQUIRED

Blueprint §8.1 correctly says:

> supplier payment state is distinct from goods-receipt state.

That distinction is mandatory.

Contract 7 §8 says a confirmed purchase **may create/associate** an inventory movement and a Ledger expense.

INV-A9 currently strengthens that into:

> “A confirmed purchase writes the movement and the Ledger expense in one command.”

There are two risks.

### **Risk 1 — `purchase` versus generic `expense`**

SB-P-1.14 explicitly preserves distinct measures:

* `purchase`;  
* `expense`.

A stock purchase must not be casually collapsed into generic `expense` if the canonical Ledger semantics classify it as `purchase`.

### **Risk 2 — goods receipt versus cash settlement**

Receipt of goods does not prove the supplier has been paid.

A purchase may be:

* paid now;  
* payable/credit;  
* partly paid;  
* settled later.

The stock-receipt event must not create an implied supplier payment.

### **Required EIS wording**

Where one confirmed business event legitimately produces both:

* an inventory receipt movement; and  
* its associated SB-P-1.14 purchase/financial fact,

those required writes commit atomically.

But:

* exact Ledger classification comes from SB-P-1.14;  
* goods receipt never means supplier payment;  
* supplier-side `owed_by_business` credit remains distinct;  
* later payment/repayment remains a separate governed event;  
* no duplicate purchase/expense/payment is created.

# **14\. Closing cash — complete / partial / unknown**

## **Finding SA03-FI-08 — Decision table is strong but COMPLETE needs affirmative completeness evidence**

**Severity:** MEDIUM  
**Disposition:** CONDITIONAL PASS WITH REFINEMENT

§§8.8 and 20.9.2 correctly reject:

* zero-filling missing inputs;  
* guessed timestamps;  
* invented operator attribution;  
* double-counted POS/manual cash;  
* final variance where actual count is missing.

The severity precedence:

> UNKNOWN → REVIEW-REQUIRED → PARTIAL → COMPLETE

is financially sensible.

### **UNKNOWN**

Appropriate when:

* period cannot be defined;  
* timezone unavailable;  
* no actual count exists for variance.

No final closing variance may be asserted.

### **REVIEW-REQUIRED**

Appropriate where:

* POS/manual overlap remains unresolved;  
* post-close correction/reversal reopens the period.

A review-required result must not feed anomaly conclusions as a settled final figure.

### **PARTIAL**

Correct where:

* configured source data is incomplete;  
* opening float is required but absent;  
* shift attribution is impossible because manual records have no time.

### **COMPLETE — required refinement**

“Every configured source has data” is not always enough.

For a complete result, the system needs evidence that the source is **complete for the period**, not merely that at least one row arrived.

Examples:

* POS API says sync watermark/cursor is current;  
* import/export declares the full interval;  
* merchant explicitly confirms manual cash entry is complete for the period;  
* no unresolved source conflict remains.

Without a completeness marker or authorized attestation, absence of another manual record is not proof that none occurred.

### **Financial presentation constraint**

Where expected cash is PARTIAL, any arithmetic against the actual count should be labelled something equivalent to:

> **difference against known inputs**

or unmistakably **partial/provisional variance**.

It must not be displayed or consumed as the same final variance used when completeness is proven.

`EIS-KER-06` recognizes `integration` as a **channel**, not a principal type.

`EIS-ID-05` allows business-scoped external integration/provider references, but those references are identity records; it does not make them authorization principals.

Therefore the Blueprint currently attributes a principal type to `EIS-KER-02` that the approved upstream architecture does not contain.

This must be resolved explicitly. SB-P-1.17 must not silently invent a fifth authority principal.

The present separation is correct.

A standing rule is **not capability 5 and is not one of the seven Manager capabilities**.

The Blueprint correctly delegates its execution mechanics to `EIS-AUT-01` while preserving Owner-only rule creation.

A revoked/expired/out-of-scope rule fails closed to a suggestion.

# **8\. Finding SA-01-F02 — integration-principal mismatch**

## **Evidence**

SB-P-1.17 §20.4.6:

> authenticated API/webhook adapter, under an integration principal on `EIS-KER-02`

§20.5 repeats:

> POS adapters authenticate as a verified integration principal.

But SB-P-1.12 `EIS-KER-02` says all authorization begins from exactly one of its four defined principal classes. No integration principal exists.

`integration` appears only as a channel under `EIS-KER-06`.

External provider references under `EIS-ID-05` are business-scoped references, not authorization principals.

## **Risk**

If left unresolved, an implementation could improvise:

* service-role-as-authority;  
* caller-supplied business identity;  
* provider credential → merchant authority;  
* an unversioned fifth principal class;  
* a parallel POS permission engine.

All would violate the locked authority architecture.

## **Required correction**

The SB-P-1.17 EIS must not proceed on the assertion that `EIS-KER-02` already supplies an integration principal.

Mission Control must choose the architecture-governed resolution path:

**Path A:** a separately governed amendment to SB-P-1.12 formally adds an integration principal, including verification, business binding, authorization inputs, serialization, revocation, audit and negative tests;

**or**

**Path B:** SB-P-1.17 models the POS intake using already-approved principal/authority primitives without inventing a new principal class.

Security Room does **not** choose Path A or B here.

The important invariant is:

> authentication of a provider proves source identity; it does not itself prove merchant authority.

A POS credential or webhook signature must never become Owner/Manager permission.

# **9\. Confirmation and execution-time authority**

The design is aligned with SB-P-1.12/1.13.

For capability 2 reorder confirmation:

1. authorized user obtains scoped suggestion;  
2. server canonicalizes item/supplier/quantity/value/current state;  
3. `confirmation_bindings` records exact actor, business, action, target, fingerprint, authority epochs and expiry;  
4. user confirms;  
5. commit re-evaluates authorization under `EIS-CNF-02`;  
6. material changes or revocation reject the stale confirmation;  
7. successful consumption is atomic and idempotent.

SB-P-1.13 adds the necessary AI protection: after confirmation the model cannot silently rewrite the proposal.

### **Additional EIS obligation**

The same binding principle must be used for any other SB-P-1.17 action that the EIS classifies as consequential.

In particular, explicit Owner historical supplier attribution must bind:

* Owner principal;  
* business;  
* original transaction;  
* proposed supplier identity;  
* reviewed transaction/supplier state;  
* expiry/version.

The words “Owner confirmation” are not sufficient implementation authority by themselves.

# **10\. Required negative-test contract**

At minimum, the SB-P-1.17 EIS must require the following.

### **Capability isolation**

1. Manager with no grants → deny capabilities 1–7.  
2. Grant capability 1 only → capabilities 2,3,4,6,7 remain denied.  
3. Repeat isolation test for every delegable capability.  
4. Item-scoped grant cannot access another item.  
5. Supplier-scoped grant cannot access another supplier.  
6. Counter-scoped grant cannot access another counter.  
7. Grant valid in Business A cannot be used in Business B.  
8. Expired/revoked grant denies at next read and cannot commit prepared work.

### **Owner-reserved capability 5**

9. Manager role alone → deny operator detail.  
10. Manager with capability 4 → still deny operator detail.  
11. Direct attempt to create a Manager capability-5 grant → deny.  
12. Direct RPC/query for operator-attributed detail → deny.  
13. Ask CFO/conversation prompt requesting operator attribution → no retrieval.  
14. Employee → no operator anomaly analysis about another staff member.

### **Standing-rule separation**

15. Capability 2 Manager attempts rule creation → deny.  
16. Manager possessing every six delegable capabilities attempts rule creation → deny.  
17. Expired/revoked rule → no execution.  
18. Rule whose Owner/grantor lost authority → no execution.  
19. Rule exceeding item/supplier/value/frequency limit → deny/fallback.  
20. Trigger payload attempting to widen limits → ignored/denied.

### **Reference Cost and margin**

21. Manager with capability 1/2/3 but no Reference Cost → supplier cost absent.  
22. Same denial through direct RPC.  
23. Same denial through export.  
24. Same denial through Ask CFO.  
25. Same denial through conversation/voice context.  
26. Same denial through cached/history content after Reference Cost revocation.  
27. Reference Cost grant → cost may appear only within object scope.  
28. Reference Cost without margin → no computed/labeled margin.  
29. Margin grant without Reference Cost → no raw cost disclosure.  
30. Neither grant → neither cost nor margin-derived output.  
31. Employee → no cost/margin.  
32. Supplier → no merchant cost history except exact merchant-authored request content if intentionally included.

### **Supplier isolation**

33. Supplier A cannot access Supplier B objects.  
34. Supplier in Business A cannot access same identifier in Business B.  
35. Supplier cannot reach dashboard endpoints/readers.  
36. Supplier cannot enumerate stock, customer, counter or closing records.  
37. Unbound supplier participant record exercises zero capability.

### **Confirmation and revocation**

38. Confirmation by another actor fails.  
39. Confirmation in another business fails.  
40. Changed item/supplier/quantity/value requires new binding.  
41. Changed reorder state invalidates old binding.  
42. Revocation after preview but before commit rejects the action.  
43. Concurrent confirmations commit exactly once.  
44. Stale conversational “yes” cannot execute.  
45. Historical supplier-link confirmation cannot be replayed against a different transaction/supplier.

### **POS / integration boundary**

46. Invalid webhook/API authentication → reject before business mutation.  
47. Valid provider credential with forged `business_id` → reject.  
48. Source mapped to Business A cannot write/read Business B.  
49. Duplicate external event → one committed outcome.  
50. Provider payload cannot specify Owner/Manager/capability/authorization decision.  
51. `service_role` possession alone creates no merchant authority.  
52. Unknown/unbound integration source cannot create stock or Ledger effects.  
53. Any future integration-principal implementation must have its own negative matrix against all human/participant businesses and scopes.

Specify exact confirmation payloads for reorder and every other consequential operation, including historical supplier attribution.

Every SB-P-1.17 conversational tool must identify:

* read/write class;  
* required capability;  
* object scope;  
* consequential status;  
* confirmation requirement.

No principal/business/grant field may come from model output.

### **`SA17-EIS-09 — Effective-access verification`**

For every new table, view, sequence and function, verify effective access for at least:

* `PUBLIC`;  
* `anon`;  
* `authenticated`;  
* service role;  
* executor roles;  
* actual creator-role default privileges.

Include exact function overload/signature EXECUTE tests.
