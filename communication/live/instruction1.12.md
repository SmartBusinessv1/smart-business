## 2. Reconciled eligibility model

A Smart Business personal identity may become eligible for deletion only when **all** of the following are satisfied:

1. **No surviving owned business:** authoritative SB-P-1.12 ownership state shows the person owns no surviving business.
2. **Final owned business actually deleted:** the formerly final owned business has completed a permitted, governed lifecycle deletion under the SB-P-1.19 lifecycle boundary; cancellation, pause, scheduled deletion, hidden state or a deletion request are not sufficient.
3. **No authorized membership remains:** authoritative SB-P-1.12 membership/authority state shows zero current authorized memberships in any other person's business.
4. **No incomplete ownership transfer:** a pending/incomplete transfer remains an ownership state and therefore blocks identity deletion until transfer completion changes ownership authoritatively.
5. **Relevant obligations satisfied:** no authoritatively established legal, privacy, retention, commercial, dispute, security or lifecycle obligation remains that genuinely requires the personal identity to stay active.
6. **History and attribution preserved lawfully:** required financial, authority, audit and business history can survive without preserving active authority. Surviving attribution must be non-authorizing and its permissible identifier form remains subject to competent legal/privacy/retention validation.
7. **Governed deletion path exists:** the deletion action is separately authorized, confirmed, recorded and protected from direct/uncontrolled cascade behavior.
8. **ESC-1 is no longer relied upon:** no identity-deletion implementation may rely on the current direct business-delete or Auth-user cascade paths while ESC-1 remains unresolved.

These are eligibility and integrity conditions. They do not authorize implementation.

## 3. Reconciled meaning of “relevant obligations”

### 3.1 Business-scoped obligations

The following are ordinarily business/subscription/lifecycle matters, not automatically obligations of the person's active Smart Business identity:

- subscription dues and account state;
- payment failures/pending events;
- refunds;
- chargebacks and disputes;
- payouts/settlements;
- reconciliation exceptions;
- lifecycle cancellation/reactivation state;
- support tickets, complaints and export requests.

Such matters may prevent the **business** from completing governed deletion or may expose another underlying obligation. They do not automatically justify retaining the person's active identity.

### 3.2 Identity-dependent obligations

A commercial or lifecycle obligation may block personal-identity deletion only where authoritative evidence establishes that:

1. the obligation remains unresolved after the business lifecycle reaches its otherwise permissible disposition;
2. it remains attributable to or actionable by that specific person;
3. completion, receipt, contest, settlement or reconciliation genuinely requires the continuing Smart Business identity; and
4. no approved durable non-identity settlement, claims, audit or case record can safely carry the obligation.

No canonical source currently establishes a universal class of such personal commercial obligations.

### 3.3 Legal/retention obligations

Identity deletion and record retention are separate lifecycle operations.

Lawfully required records may survive identity deletion under their applicable legal, tax, accounting, security, audit, dispute or other documented retention basis. Surviving records do not by themselves justify retaining the person's full live Smart Business profile.

Founder-approved 90-day business lifecycle periods are **not** legal retention periods for personal identity data.

### 3.4 Support/lifecycle items

An open ticket, complaint, export request or support escalation is not an automatic identity-deletion blocker. It matters only where it proves that an underlying approved lifecycle, commercial, legal, security or integrity obligation remains unresolved.
