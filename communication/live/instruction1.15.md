## 2. Mission Control research disposition

Mission Control accepts the canonical research report as **SUFFICIENT FOR PROVISIONAL PHASE ONE R-2 SOURCE-ACCOUNTING USE, WITH MATERIAL CONDITIONS AND PRESERVED UNCERTAINTIES**.

The report materially supports the MC-109 working architecture, with conditions.

Accepted Phase One conclusions are:

1. **Identity deletion and record retention are separate lifecycle operations.**
2. **No universal retention period applies to all Smart Business data.**
3. **Founder-approved 90-day business lifecycle periods are operational lifecycle rules, not statutory retention periods.**
4. **Merchant statutory records may survive account/identity deletion where their legal/business retention purpose remains.**
5. **The existence of retained statutory/audit records does not by itself require the full live Smart Business identity to remain active.**
6. **Surviving personal data should be minimized to the purpose that justifies retention.**
7. **Historical attribution may survive only as a non-authorizing relationship; reversible pseudonymization remains personal-data processing.**
8. **A minimal deletion-proof/tombstone may be used as a bounded accountability mechanism, but no universal statutory tombstone requirement or universal retention period was established.**
9. **Backups must not silently resurrect deleted identity state; exact immutable-backup expiry remains professionally deferred.**
10. **Support tickets and routine complaints are not blanket grounds to retain a full active identity.**
11. **Legal/dispute holds should be scoped to affected records and basis rather than implemented as indefinite full-profile retention by default.**
12. **Owner identity deletion must not cascade-delete independent employee, statutory, financial, authority or audit records whose retention purpose survives.**

## 3. Current-law / future-law handling for Phase One

The research identifies materially different legal regimes and effective dates.

Mission Control therefore prohibits a single global retention duration from being used as the R-2 source requirement.

For Phase One source accounting:

- GST, income-tax, company-law, security-log and other retention obligations must remain **record-class and applicability dependent**;
- future-effective DPDP requirements should shape forward-compatible design assumptions, but must not be represented as already-operative duties where the research says they are future-effective;
- classification-dependent obligations, including intermediary status and certain CERT-In provider categories, must remain explicitly conditional;
- unresolved Kerala employment/payroll/attendance periods must remain unresolved rather than receiving an invented number.

No Phase One source text may claim a universal statutory retention period unless the canonical research or later professional validation supports it.

## 4. Merchant versus Team LIPS responsibility

The research supports the distinction that statutory duties attaching to the merchant/taxpayer/company do not automatically transfer to Team LIPS merely because Smart Business hosts the records.

For Phase One source accounting:

- merchant-owned statutory duties and Team LIPS-owned independent duties must be represented separately;
- Smart Business may operationally host/export/archive records needed by the merchant;
- Team LIPS may also have independent tax, cybersecurity, contract, privacy or platform duties depending on its actual legal classification;
- the source model must not imply that Team LIPS extinguishes a merchant's statutory preservation duty by hosting the data.

The precise legal allocation remains professionally deferred where the report marks classification or contractual treatment unresolved.

## 5. Personal-data disposition rule for R-2

Mission Control adopts the following **provisional Phase One source-accounting rule**:

When a Smart Business personal identity becomes eligible for deletion, active authentication/profile data and personal data whose purpose has ended should be erased or irreversibly anonymized where the applicable basis permits, while personal data embedded in records that must lawfully or operationally survive may remain only to the minimum extent required by the surviving record's legitimate purpose.

This rule is provisional and research-backed. It is not a final legal retention policy.

A retained pseudonymous identifier that Team LIPS can relink to a person must continue to be treated as personal data for source-accounting purposes.

## 6. Reconciled “relevant obligations” definition

For Phase One R-2 source accounting, “relevant obligations” means obligations that are both:

1. **authoritatively established** under an applicable legal, commercial, lifecycle, security, claims, dispute or retention basis; and
2. **material to identity deletion**, meaning the obligation genuinely requires either:
   - the personal identity to remain active; or
   - specified retained personal data / historical attribution to survive independently after the active identity is deleted.

This does **not** mean every retained record blocks identity deletion.

The following distinctions remain mandatory:

- business-scoped obligation versus identity-dependent obligation;
- record retention versus active identity retention;
- legal hold versus normal retention;
- support/case status versus underlying obligation;
- pseudonymous historical attribution versus live authority.
