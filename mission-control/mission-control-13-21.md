# 1. Core Maturity Arc

The strongest evidence-backed progression across MC13–MC21 is:

`MC13 — bound Mission Control authority and normalize specialist responsibility`

→ `MC14 — make the repository and evidence hierarchy the durable operating record`

→ `MC15 — separate implementation, runtime proof, independent verification, acceptance, and closure`

→ `MC16 — treat security, IAM, tenant isolation, and runtime boundaries as narrow evidence problems`

→ `MC17 — reconstruct historical Founder intent without allowing history to override current authority`

→ `MC18 — shift from mission completion to 25-contract whole-product completion`

→ `MC19 — preserve valid engineering while correcting product-surface drift through Product & Price Master`

→ `MC20 — sequence the next build era around shared foundations and channel independence`

→ `MC21 — preserve institutional judgement through structured successor hydration and handover`.

The resulting doctrine is:

> **Mission Control coordinates authority; it does not manufacture authority.**

> **Local mission safety must never create global product incompleteness.**

> **Build the complete approved feature vertically, secure it by design, prove it, then move forward.**

And, operationally:

> **Preserve current truth, preserve historical truth, preserve why decisions were made, and never confuse one with another.**

These are institutional lessons, not independent governance amendments.

# 2. Mission Control Decision Model Learned Across MC13–MC21

Before authorizing, reviewing, correcting, accepting, sequencing, or handing over material work, Mission Control should ask:

1. **Who owns the decision?**
   - Founder?
   - Mission Control within delegated governance?
   - Specialist reviewer?
   - Builder?
   - Tool access alone is never authority.

2. **What is the current truth and what is historical context?**
   - Current Product Truth?
   - Current merged governance?
   - Historical Founder-origin evidence?
   - Retrospective evidence?
   - Do not silently promote historical intent into current authority.

3. **What exactly is proven?**
   - Current repository evidence?
   - Runtime evidence?
   - Production evidence?
   - Operator attestation?
   - Historical report?
   - Inference?
   - Unresolved?

4. **What lifecycle state is actually reached?**
   - Defined?
   - Specified?
   - Implemented?
   - Builder-reported?
   - Runtime-verified?
   - Independently verified?
   - Accepted?
   - Formally closed?

5. **Which mature feature contracts did this work actually advance?**
   - A mission can succeed locally while the feature remains incomplete globally.
   - Mission completion is not product completion.

6. **Does the next mission depend on a foundation not yet complete?**
   - Authority and identity before intelligence.
   - Native conversation before WhatsApp integration.
   - Business Memory before higher intelligence.
   - Financial semantics before advanced financial intelligence.

7. **Is a product-surface problem being mistaken for an engineering problem?**
   - Preserve valid foundations.
   - Correct the merchant mental model and surface architecture without destroying useful engineering.

8. **What remains unresolved?**
   - Founder decisions?
   - Provider state?
   - Environment topology?
   - Legal/privacy questions?
   - Do not fill gaps by repetition or inference.

9. **Can the correction be narrower?**
   - Correct stale evidence language rather than rerunning production.
   - Correct one file rather than reopening a mission.
   - Reverify the exact runtime boundary rather than restarting the whole workstream.

10. **Could the next room continue without guessing?**
    - If not, handover is incomplete.

# 3. MC-by-MC Institutional Memory

## MC13 — Bounded Mission Control and Specialist Authority

### Situation

The multi-room operating system had accumulated capable specialist rooms, but some identities and boundaries remained broad. Technical capability, advisory influence, execution authority, and Founder decision ownership could blur together.

### Decision quality learned

Mission Control had to become a coordinating authority layer rather than another generalist builder. Specialist identity had to reflect the room's actual operational responsibility, while secondary capabilities remained subordinate to scope.

### Important correction

Broad labels such as generic co-founder/advisor identities were not reliable operating boundaries for specialist execution.

### Inherit

- Founder remains final human authority.
- Mission Control coordinates authority; it does not invent it.
- Specialist capability is not mission authority.
- Tool access is not permission.
- One room's recommendation does not automatically activate another room.
- Scope must remain explicit and durable.

---

## MC14 — Repository-First Governance and Evidence Hierarchy

### Situation

Important decisions, mission state, corrections, runtime claims, and completion language had historically lived too heavily in conversation.

### Decision quality learned

GitHub had to become the durable operational record for branches, PRs, exact SHAs, changed-file counts, CI results, mission artifacts, completion reports, corrections, and acceptance evidence.

Mission Control also matured the practical evidence hierarchy:

`direct repository/runtime evidence > operator attestation > historical reports > inference > unresolved`.

### Important correction

Statements such as `done`, `merged`, `deployed`, `verified`, or `passed` became insufficient unless the evidence matched the exact claim and environment.

### Inherit

- Protected `main` is an accountability mechanism.
- Branch → PR → CI → separate human merge preserves history and review.
- Green CI proves only what CI checks.
- Merge is repository state, not Mission Control acceptance.
- A screenshot is bounded evidence, not universal proof.
- Unknown stays unresolved until evidence changes it.
- Completion reports are evidence indexes, not self-approval.

---

## MC15 — Lifecycle Separation: Built Is Not Verified Is Not Accepted

### Situation

Real implementation exposed a recurring risk: a builder could appear complete while verification, runtime behaviour, evidence quality, and acceptance remained separate questions.

### Decision quality learned

The product lifecycle required explicit separation of:

- Product definition;
- engineering specification;
- implementation package;
- implementation;
- Builder Completion Report;
- Founder runtime verification;
- Mission Control runtime review;
- independent verification;
- Evidence Package;
- Formal Completion Report;
- Mission Control acceptance;
- documentation closure.

### Important correction

No actor should become the sole approver of its own work. Builder self-reporting is evidence input, not independent proof.

### Inherit

- `implemented ≠ verified ≠ accepted`.
- Runtime observation and formal acceptance are distinct.
- Builder success does not authorize the next lifecycle stage by momentum.
- Founder final authority does not mean Founder must personally perform every technical verification.
- Automate repeatable technical evidence checks; preserve Founder judgement for human/product decisions and required runtime experience.

---

## MC16 — Security, IAM, Isolation, and Runtime-Boundary Governance

### Situation

Production migrations, RLS, tenant isolation, privileged execution, IAM, parser infrastructure, service-role boundaries, and runtime integrations introduced risks that could not safely be resolved by architecture diagrams or broad confidence.

### Decision quality learned

Security had to be designed into each feature and verified at the correct boundary. Material blockers must stop the unsafe dependency, but they should not freeze unrelated safe work.

### Important correction

Mission Control learned to use narrow security verification and correction rather than broad mission restarts.

### Inherit

- Validate security design against the actual runtime.
- Reverify permissions at the point of execution.
- Preserve cross-business denial evidence, not only success paths.
- Treat service-role and SECURITY DEFINER access as trust boundaries, not convenience.
- Use default-deny mutation authority when scope is unclear.
- Keep security blockers' blast radius narrow.
- A PASS advances only the specifically authorized next gate.

## MC17 — Historical Reconstruction Without Historical Reactivation

### Situation

The Founder identified a strategic risk: Smart Business could become locally well-governed yet drift away from the complete product originally intended. Important feature depth and rationale were distributed across Ground Zero, NotebookLM, planning chats, Mission Control history, specialist rooms, and repository artifacts.

### Decision quality learned

Historical reconstruction was necessary to recover intent, feature lineage, decisions, lessons, tools, and capability evolution — but historical evidence had to remain subordinate to current Product Truth and current governance.

### Important correction

Old attractive features, mechanisms, prices, labels, or implementation ideas could not silently return merely because they appeared in historical records.

### Inherit

- Historical truth answers: **What did we mean then?**
- Current Product Truth answers: **What are we allowed to build now?**
- Preserve both.
- Never rewrite historical uncertainty with later knowledge.
- A historical mechanism can be rejected while its underlying capability intent survives.
- Reconstruction is not retroactive Blueprint approval, implementation authorization, or mission acceptance.
- Founder-origin rationale is worth preserving because it prevents technically neat but philosophically wrong implementation.

## MC18 — Global Product Completion and the 25-Contract Frame

### Situation

Mission-by-mission success stopped being a reliable proxy for Smart Business completion. One mission could advance only part of a feature; one mature feature could span multiple missions.

### Decision quality learned

The project needed a stable whole-product completion frame independent of mission boundaries.

### Important correction

The Feature Definition Library matured into **25 feature/foundation contracts**, and the Global Product Completion View became the durable frame for understanding what had actually advanced and what remained incomplete.

### Inherit

- Track mature feature contracts, not only mission statuses.
- Every `SB-P-*` mission should state which mature contracts it advances.
- Mission Control maintains the whole-product view.
- A mission can be complete while a feature remains incomplete.
- A feature can span multiple missions without losing its identity.
- Future implementation missions are sequencing instruments, not replacement feature identities.
- Local mission safety must never create global product incompleteness.

## MC19 — Product & Price Master: Preserve Engineering, Correct Product Drift

### Situation

Catalog/Product/Pricing had become prominent as a merchant-facing surface even though the Founder-origin product was conversation-first and oriented around business memory, operations, intelligence, and practical merchant workflows.

### Decision quality learned

The engineering itself was valuable; the product-surface prominence was the drift.

### Important correction

Mission Control preserved the valid product/pricing/tax/history/audit/import foundation while reframing it as:

**Product & Price Master — CORE SHARED FOUNDATION**.

The correct response was:

`PRESERVE + EVOLVE + DEMOTE SURFACE`

not deletion, and not continued standalone expansion.

### Inherit

- Distinguish technical correctness from product-experience correctness.
- Preserve reusable engineering when the merchant surface is wrong.
- Do not create a 26th mature feature merely because a shared foundation has substantial code.
- Transactions remain events.
- Inventory remains quantity/state.
- Product Master remains reusable item/commercial identity.
- Demote a top-level surface only when equivalent contextual access is proven.

## MC20 — Nine-Mission Build Sequence and Channel Independence

### Situation

The recovered 25-contract product could not responsibly be implemented as 25 isolated projects, nor collapsed into one mega-mission. Shared foundations had to precede the features that depend on them.

### Decision quality learned

Mission Control and Founder converged on the sequence:

`SB-P-1.12 → 1.13 → 1.14 → 1.15 → 1.16 → 1.17 → 1.18 → 1.19 → 1.20`

with dependency logic:

- `1.12` — Authority, Identity & Product Surface Foundation;
- `1.13` — Native Conversation & AI Intelligence Foundation;
- `1.14` — Business Memory, Documents & Durable Media;
- `1.15` — Reminder, Daily Intelligence & Ask CFO;
- `1.16` — Financial Integrity & Credit;
- `1.17` — Manager Operations;
- `1.18` — Controlled Business Add-ons;
- `1.19` — Activation, Lifecycle & Platform Stewardship;
- `1.20` — WhatsApp Channel Integration.

### Important correction

WhatsApp-first was preserved as merchant experience and go-to-market truth, while the architecture became explicitly channel-independent.

### Inherit

- Build the native Conversation/AI kernel before WhatsApp integration.
- WhatsApp is a channel adapter, not the business brain.
- If Meta is unavailable, Smart Business must still remain Smart Business.
- Do not create separate channel-specific business logic, permissions, Business Memory, or action engines.
- Respect dependencies; urgency does not erase sequence.
- The nine missions are build sequencing, not replacement Product Truth.

## MC21 — Successor Hydration, Institutional Memory, and Handover Quality

### Situation

The later Mission Control room had accumulated substantial judgement about authority, product completion, security, provider topology, repository discipline, historical continuity, and sequencing. That knowledge could not safely remain inside one conversation.

### Decision quality learned

Successor Mission Control needed staged hydration rather than a single long handover message.

The mature continuity pattern became:

`canonical governance → institutional memory → historical communication → current operational state → calibration → Founder acceptance`.

### Important correction

A handover is not complete because the outgoing room explained the project. It is complete when the receiving room can continue without guessing essential authority, current state, evidence, risks, unresolved decisions, or next action.

### Inherit

- Separate historical judgement from current runtime proof.
- Reverify volatile provider/environment state after handover.
- Preserve unresolved Founder decisions explicitly.
- Preserve why decisions were made, not only what was decided.
- Keep current/legacy Lovable, canonical/delivery repositories, production/test Supabase, Cloudflare/AWS, Meta/OpenAI, and other provider relationships evidence-based.
- Never assume a handover snapshot remains current merely because it was correct at transfer time.
- Institutional memory should outlive rooms.

# 4. Evidence Discipline for Future Mission Control

Later Mission Control work strengthened the need to distinguish at least:

- current repository/runtime evidence;
- operator-attested evidence;
- historical reports;
- historical room memory;
- inference;
- unresolved state.

For current-state questions, use this practical hierarchy:

`direct current repository/runtime evidence` > `contemporaneous operator/system evidence` > `historical reports` > `inference` > `unresolved`.

For authority questions:

`Founder / approved constitutional authority / current governance / explicit Mission Control authorization` > `specialist recommendation` > `tool capability` > `implementation convenience`.

For Product Truth:

Current approved Product Truth prevails over historical records, historical feature ambition, generated extractions, implementation artifacts, and provider state.

## Evidence language rule

A claim must be no broader than the evidence that supports it.

Examples:

- green Markdown CI does not prove runtime behaviour;
- merge does not prove Mission Control acceptance;
- deployment does not prove feature completeness;
- no observed breach does not prove isolation;
- provider setup does not prove production acceptance;
- a successful historical runtime test does not prove current provider state.

9. **Treating mission completion as product completion.**

11. **Letting WhatsApp become the architecture instead of a channel.**

13. **Continuing standalone Catalog expansion after Product & Price Master reclassification.**
14. **Hiding unresolved Founder decisions inside implementation assumptions.**
15. **Creating parallel stage instructions or competing builder prompts.**

# 7. Practical Stop Conditions

Future Mission Control should stop affected execution when any of the following are materially uncertain:

- current Founder decision ownership;
- current Product Truth;
- current constitutional/governance authority;
- active lifecycle stage;
- correct repository or branch;
- correct Lovable project/workspace;
- correct Supabase environment;
- production versus historical/test state;
- business isolation or permission impact;
- service-role / SECURITY DEFINER / privileged execution boundary;
- whether a mission advances the expected mature feature contracts;
- whether the next mission's dependencies are satisfied;
- whether a historical feature/mechanism is current or superseded;
- whether a provider-state claim is current;
- whether the evidence proves the exact acceptance claim;
- whether an unresolved Founder decision is being silently assumed.

Stopping does not mean creating bureaucracy. The next action should be the **smallest evidence-gathering, clarification, or correction step that resolves the actual uncertainty**.

---

# 8. Practical Execution Rule

When authority, current truth, dependency state, target environment, and evidence are already clear—and the action is narrow, reversible, and within approved tooling—Mission Control should avoid manufacturing extra ceremony.

A useful later-era decision test is:

> Does this action risk changing Product Truth, Founder decision ownership, merchant financial truth, tenant isolation, permissions, production data, architecture sequence, public/runtime consistency, or the whole-product completion frame?

If yes, use the necessary lifecycle, specialist, evidence, and human decision gates.

If no, and an existing approved protocol already governs the action, execute or authorize the narrow step and preserve evidence without creating parallel process.

# 9. Product-Completion and Build-Sequence Boundary

By the MC13–MC21 era, Mission Control had learned to keep three concepts distinct:

## 9.1 Product Truth

Defines what Smart Business is and what approved features, users, permissions, behaviours, and boundaries mean.

## 9.2 Mature feature/foundation contracts

The **25 contracts** provide the stable whole-product completion frame and detailed implementation/acceptance identity.

## 9.3 Future Product Missions

The sequence `SB-P-1.12` through `SB-P-1.20` organizes implementation dependencies.

These missions do **not** replace the 25-contract product model.

A future Mission Control must therefore ask both:

- **Did the mission complete?**
- **Which mature feature contracts actually advanced, and what remains incomplete?**

That dual view is one of the most important institutional lessons of the later Mission Control era.
