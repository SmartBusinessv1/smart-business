# Smart Business Feature Definition — Human Language Layer

**Status:** NOTEBOOKLM SEED — DEEP EXTRACTION REQUIRED  
**Build commitment:** BUILD NOW  
**Commercial availability:** Core cross-feature capability  
**Authority boundary:** Founder-feature elaboration / Product Blueprint input. Not yet implementation-ready.

---

## 1. Feature Identity

The Human Language Layer allows every approved Smart Business user to interact in the language that feels natural to them.

Supported languages:

- English
- Malayalam
- Manglish

NotebookLM briefing confirms this applies individually across:

- Owner
- Manager
- Employee
- Delivery Staff
- Supplier
- Customer

---

## 2. Product Principle

Language should reduce friction and preserve dignity.

The system must understand Kerala business meaning rather than produce literal robotic translation.

Business wisdom remains with the human; language is only the bridge.

---

## 3. Cross-Feature Scope

Language preference should affect, where applicable:

- WhatsApp conversation;
- dashboard / Conversation Workspace;
- transaction confirmation;
- Ask CFO;
- reminders;
- Daily Intelligence;
- Staff / HR communication;
- supplier communication;
- customer order/delivery communication;
- support automation;
- voice interaction.

---

## 4. Current Seed Behaviour

Current Product Truth supports:

- language selection during onboarding;
- later language change through WhatsApp;
- later language change through dashboard;
- natural Malayalam rather than robotic translation.

The deeper NotebookLM extraction must recover the original examples, user-specific language persistence and conversational switching rules.

---

## 5. Voice Relationship

The Human Language Layer is shared by Basic Voice and Smart Voice Assistant Plus.

Voice should not become a separate language system.

The same user preference and identity should inform text and voice while preserving privacy and role restrictions.

---

## 6. Unresolved Deep-Extraction Questions

NotebookLM must still recover:

- original Malayalam and Manglish examples;
- local business vocabulary and meanings;
- automatic language detection vs explicit preference;
- code-switching behaviour;
- per-user language persistence;
- fallback language behaviour;
- language handling in generated reports/exports;
- language behaviour for suppliers/customers without accounts;
- voice pronunciation/personality rules;
- multilingual support escalation;
- legal/official wording boundaries.

See NotebookLM question bank Questions 35–39.

---

## 7. Completion Gate

This layer must be defined deeply enough that later feature builders do not implement Malayalam/Manglish as a superficial translation toggle.
